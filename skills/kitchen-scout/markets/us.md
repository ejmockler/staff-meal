# Market: United States

## Local palate vector
Sweeter, bigger portions, cheese and sour cream added, mild by default. "Spicy" on a menu means about 4 on the heat ladder. Many kitchens keep a "native hot" level ("Indian hot", "Thai hot") for customers who ask in those words. Ask for it only when the brief's heat is at or above the dish's native top level. Otherwise ask for the dish's usual home heat, not the maximum.

## Counter and line languages
English at the counter. **Line cooks across many cuisines are often Spanish-speaking**, whatever cuisine the kitchen serves. A short Spanish line (for example "como lo hacen en casa, sin adaptar", meaning "the way it's made at home, unadapted") can land even at a Thai or Indian place. Notes: English + the cuisine's language, plus Spanish where it fits.

## Platforms (as of 2026-11; re-verify after 6 months)
- **DoorDash**: the largest. Also owns Caviar.
- **Uber Eats**: second. Postmates now runs on Uber's network rather than as a separate competitor. Field notes: `platforms/ubereats.md`.
- **Grubhub**: smaller, owned by Wonder since January 2025. Called Seamless in NYC.
- **Fantuan (饭团外卖)** and **HungryPanda (熊猫外卖)**: check these for Chinese and wider Asian kitchens in Flushing, SGV, the Bay Area, Seattle, Boston, Houston, Las Vegas and similar hubs. They often carry the Chinese-language or "traditional" menu, and kitchens that aren't on the big three.
- **Recent exits:** Postmates as an independent company (absorbed by Uber). Waitr/ASAP, a Gulf South regional app, shut down in 2024.

## Proxy flips
- Halal-cart or "Mediterranean" places are often Egyptian-, Afghan- or Bangladeshi-run (NYC carts are mostly Egyptian). Order koshari or kabuli pulao if listed. Levantine "Mediterranean" (Palestinian, Lebanese, Syrian, Jordanian) is in-culture for those cuisines.
- "Indian" restaurants are often **Nepali- or Bangladeshi-run** (also Pakistani-run). A momo, thukpa or sekuwa section means it's in-culture for Nepali; bhorta, shatkora or kala bhuna means Bangladeshi.
- "Thai" restaurants are often **Lao-run**, especially in the Upper Midwest, Central Valley and Pacific Northwest. A Lao section (larb with khao khua, tam mak hoong, sai oua, sticky rice in baskets) means it's in-culture for Lao.
- "Mexican" places are often **Salvadoran-, Guatemalan- or Ecuadorian-run**. Pupusas, curtido, or Guatemalan/Ecuadorian specials mean it's in-culture for that cuisine.
- Chinese-American takeout, hibachi and sushi buffets are often **Fuzhounese-run**. Look for Fuzhou dishes (fish balls, red-wine-lees chicken) on a Chinese-language menu.
- NYC-area pizzerias and red-sauce Italian places are often **Albanian/Kosovar-run**. Sometimes burek or tavë appear as specials.

## Hard-filter notes
- **Pork** is everywhere and is not flagged by default. Lard turns up in refried beans, tamales and some tortillas, and pork stock in ramen and some Chinese soups. Ask, or pick kitchens that state otherwise.
- **Halal** has no federal standard. Certification comes from private bodies (for example IFANCA, HFSAA) and is often self-declared. "Halal" on an app tag may cover only the meat, not shared fryers or alcohol in sauces. Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Afghan, Arab, Somali and halal-cart kitchens are the strongest bets.
- **Kosher** is certified by private agencies (OU, OK, Star-K, local va'ads). Look for the agency name, not just the word "kosher".
- **Alcohol** in cooking (wine in Italian and French sauces, mirin and sake in Japanese, Shaoxing wine in Chinese) is not labeled. Alcohol delivery needs ID at the door and is legal only in some states.
- **Labeling:** restaurant menus are exempt from FDA allergen labeling. Chains with 20+ locations must post calories, but not ingredients. Treat app dietary tags as the restaurant's self-report and for a serious allergy the user must confirm with the restaurant directly; a note is not confirmation.
