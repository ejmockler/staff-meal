# Market: India

The drift here runs **two ways**:
1. **Foreign cuisines Indianized**: spicier, with paneer and veg versions, masala added, extra cheese ("cheese burst"), and Jain or no-onion-garlic variants.
2. **Cross-regional Indian drift**: a Maharashtrian dish cooked in Delhi, or a South Indian dish in Kolkata, gets the local region's palate. "North Indian" in the South and "South Indian" in the North are often generic.

## Local palate vector
It depends on the region. Gujarat (and parts of Maharashtra) runs sweet-sour; Andhra, Telangana and Chettinad run hot; Kerala runs coconut and tamarind/kudampuli-sour; Punjab runs dairy- and ghee-rich; Bengal runs mustard-forward with a balancing pinch of sugar (East Bengali cooking is hotter). Foreign food skews toward more chili, more salt, extra cheese, and veg-first.

For an Indian regional dish, the home region's level is the target, not the local city's: Gujarati dal keeps its sugar, and Andhra food keeps its heat. For a foreign dish, the extra chili is the drift to undo. Set the heat level from the brief and the dish's native default, and never just pick the hottest option.

## Counter and line languages
Hindi and/or the state language plus English. The cook's home state matters. Udupi and Kundapur (Kannada/Tulu) cooks run most vegetarian South Indian and darshini chains. Malayali cooks run Kerala restaurants and shawarma or 'hotel' joints. Bengali, Odia, Nepali, Garhwali/Kumaoni and Bihari cooks fill many North Indian, Mughlai and Indo-Chinese lines across cities, and Northeast Indian cooks fill many momo, Korean and pan-Asian kitchens.

## Platforms (as of 2026-11; re-verify after 6 months)
- **Majors:** Swiggy and Zomato (Zomato is owned by Eternal). Both have veg-only modes (Zomato Pure Veg, Swiggy Veg Mode); use them for Jain or veg briefs.
- **Secondary:** magicpin (on ONDC) and Rapido's Ownly (zero-commission, folded into the main Rapido app in mid-2026; started in Bengaluru, and Hyderabad followed). Ownly lists magicpin's restaurants through a partnership. Check these when a small, community-run kitchen isn't on the big two.
- **Recent exits:** Zomato shut its 10-minute "Quick" service and Swiggy shut "Snacc" (2025). Zomato "Legends" (intercity food) closed in 2024. Swiggy's Bolt fast-delivery tier is still live.

## Proxy flips
- **Delhi state guest-house canteens** (Andhra Bhavan, Maharashtra Sadan, Kerala House, Nagaland House and others) serve some of the most in-culture regional food in the city. Most are dine-in only, so use them as a benchmark or for pickup, and check the app before promising delivery.
- **Kolkata's Tangra and Tiretta Bazaar "Chinese"** places are a poor match for mainland Chinese food, but they are the home of Hakka-Indian Chinese: chilli chicken, Hakka noodles, and the Sunday-morning Tiretta breakfast.
- **Malayali shawarma and 'hotel' joints** are a poor match for Levantine shawarma, but they are in-culture for Kerala and Malabar food: parotta with beef or chicken fry, Malabar biryani, porotta-kurma.
- **Udupi and darshini chains** are a generic "South Indian" for Tamil or Andhra food, but they are in-culture for coastal Karnataka vegetarian tiffin: Udupi masala dosa, neer dosa, Mangalorean buns, sambar with a touch of jaggery.
- **Irani cafes** (Mumbai, Pune, Hyderabad) are not Persian cuisine, but they are in-culture for Irani-cafe and Parsi food: bun maska, keema pav, berry pulao, Irani chai, Osmania biscuits.
- **Northeast-run "Korean" or pan-Asian kitchens** (Humayunpur in Delhi, Kammanahalli and Koramangala in Bengaluru) often carry Naga, Manipuri or Mizo dishes on the same menu. Those are in-culture even when the Korean is not: smoked pork with bamboo shoot, axone, eromba.

## Hard-filter notes
- **Pork:** it is common in Goa, the Northeast, Kodagu (pandi curry), and Goan, Mangalorean and Kerala Christian kitchens. Elsewhere it is mostly limited to Indo-Chinese, continental and Northeast-run places. Muslim-run kitchens do not serve it, and many mainstream restaurants simply leave it off.
- **Halal:** no government body certifies it. Private bodies do (for example Jamiat Ulama-i-Hind Halal Trust). Labels on the apps are self-declared by the restaurant. Most chicken and mutton in many cities comes from halal butchers by default, while Sikh-run and some Hindu-run North Indian places use jhatka. Uttar Pradesh banned sales of halal-certified products in Nov 2023 (exports exempt), so certificates are rarely shown there. For a strict brief, prefer Muslim-run kitchens over a "halal" tag.
- **Veg labeling:** the apps tag veg and non-veg with the FSSAI-style green and brown/red dot. Egg counts as **non-veg** in India, and some places mark it separately as "egg". Jain means no onion, garlic or root vegetables. The Pure Veg and Veg Mode filters exclude whole restaurants that also cook meat, which matters for strict briefs.
- **Alcohol:** food apps do not deliver it. Gujarat, Bihar, Nagaland, Mizoram and Lakshadweep are dry. Alcohol as a cooking ingredient is rare, apart from Goan and Christian dishes and Christmas plum cake, so ask about it for those.
- **Beef:** the rules differ by state (see Gaps).

## Gaps
Beef laws differ by state. Beef is generally available in Kerala, Goa, West Bengal and most of the Northeast. Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu ban cow slaughter but allow buffalo. Karnataka (2020 Act) bans cattle and young buffalo. Gujarat, UP and MP are strict. In many cities, Kerala-run places serve buffalo as 'beef fry' (BDF = beef dry fry). Accept it, check the platform listing, and never push a kitchen to break local law.
