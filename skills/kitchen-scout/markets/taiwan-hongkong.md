# Market: Taiwan and Hong Kong (and Macau)

## Local palate vector
**Taiwan:** sweet-leaning and mild. Savory dishes often carry sugar (肉燥, 三杯, 滷味), and the south (Tainan above all) is sweeter than Taipei. That sweetness is native for Taiwanese dishes, not drift. Heat is low by default and usually added at the table from a chili jar, so a 小辣 / 中辣 / 大辣 selector is common. Foreign food gets sweeter, milder and thicker sauces, set-meal framing (套餐 with soup, a drink and a small dessert), Japanese-style plating, and cheese or mayo add-ons. Sugar and ice are separate ordering axes for drinks (甜度: 無糖 / 微糖 / 半糖 / 少糖 / 正常; 冰塊: 去冰 / 少冰 / 正常). Leave the shop's standard (正常) unless the eater says otherwise.

**Hong Kong:** Cantonese 清淡 (light, fresh, clean), wok hei, salt and umami over heat or sweetness. Foreign food drifts milder, sweeter and less funky (less fish sauce and shrimp paste, less chili, coconut-sweet curries). HK-style Western food (茶餐廳: macaroni soup, baked pork chop rice, milk tea with evaporated milk) is its own cuisine, not drift. Spice selectors are normal (小辣 / 中辣 / 大辣, or numbered levels at 米線 chains). Portions are moderate, and rice is generous.

**Macau:** Cantonese base plus Macanese (土生葡人) fusion: turmeric, coconut, bacalhau, chouriço, sweet-sour stews. "Portuguese food" in Macau is often Macanese.

## Counter and line languages
**Taiwan:** Mandarin is the ticket language, written in **traditional characters**. Never write notes in simplified characters, which read as mainland and can land badly. Taiwanese Hokkien (台語) is widely spoken in kitchens, especially in the south and at old family shops. Hakka is spoken in Hsinchu, Miaoli and Meinong (Kaohsiung), but a Mandarin note works there too. Southeast Asian kitchens are run by 新住民 (immigrant spouses, many Vietnamese) or migrant workers (Indonesian, Vietnamese, Filipino, Thai). For those kitchens, add the cook's language as a line and keep Mandarin as the counter line. Polite Mandarin form: 麻煩…，謝謝. In any note or search, say 新住民 (new residents) and 移工 (migrant workers). Never use 外籍新娘 or 外勞, which read as demeaning.

**Hong Kong:** Cantonese is spoken, and tickets are read in standard written Chinese (traditional characters). Colloquial written Cantonese (唔該, 唔要) is fine and friendly, and 走 means "without" on HK tickets (走蔥 no scallion, 走冰 no ice). Use traditional characters; switch to simplified only for a mainland-run kitchen whose own menu is in simplified. English is widely read at the counter, especially at non-Chinese restaurants. Line cooks at Chinese restaurants are increasingly mainland-born, and they read the same characters. South Asian kitchens (Chungking Mansions, Jordan) run in Urdu, Hindi, Punjabi or Nepali, with English as the shared language. Match the cook's own language: Urdu (Nastaliq script) for a Pakistani kitchen and Hindi (Devanagari) for an Indian one, and never one for the other. If you can't tell, write English only. Thai kitchens in Kowloon City are Thai-run. Indonesian and Filipino shops are run by and for the domestic-worker communities, so use Bahasa Indonesia or Tagalog plus English.

**Macau:** Cantonese and Mandarin. Portuguese is official but rarely spoken in kitchens. Many service and kitchen staff are Filipino, Vietnamese, Burmese or mainland Chinese.

## Platforms (as of 2026-11; re-verify after 6 months)
- **Taiwan: Uber Eats and foodpanda** are the only two major apps (a duopoly). foodpanda Taiwan's ownership is unsettled:
  - Uber agreed to buy foodpanda Taiwan in May 2024. The Fair Trade Commission blocked it in December 2024, and Uber terminated the deal in March 2025.
  - Grab agreed in March 2026 to buy foodpanda Taiwan for US$600m. The FTC blocked that too, on 2026-10-21, citing Uber's stake in Grab. The parties may reapply.
  - foodpanda Taiwan is **not** part of Uber's 2026-07-16 agreement to acquire Delivery Hero: Delivery Hero had already agreed to sell it to Grab, and the Uber deal's Asian foodpanda markets do not include Taiwan. For now it stays a Delivery Hero business and the app runs as usual. Expect another sale attempt, and re-check before relying on any foodpanda field notes.
  - Both apps have memberships (Uber One; foodpanda's pandapro, name current as of 2026-07; benefits change, so read them at checkout). Verify membership benefits on the checkout page, never assume them.
- **Hong Kong: foodpanda and Keeta** (Meituan's app, launched May 2023) are the two major apps.
  - **Exits:** Deliveroo left on 2025-04-07 and sold selected assets (customers, riders, some restaurants) to foodpanda. Uber Eats left at the end of 2021. Do not route to either.
  - foodpanda Hong Kong **is** in the Uber–Delivery Hero deal (closing pending regulatory approval, expected in the second half of 2027). The app is unchanged for now.
  - The Competition Commission accepted Keeta's commitments on exclusivity on 2026-06-17 (restaurants can list on other platforms without losing Keeta's lower commission; no price-parity clauses). The commitments run to 2026-12-28. Exclusive listings are loosening, so check both apps for the target kitchen.
- **Macau:** the local apps **mFood** and **Aomi** lead. Both are largely Chinese-language. Uber Eats does not operate there. Keeta in Macau: (unverified, no evidence found).

## Proxy flips
- **HK 雲南米線 chains (譚仔, 三哥):** a poor stand-in for Yunnan food, but in-culture for HK's own 米線 genre. Order the house soup and pick the level on the chain's own numbered scale that matches the brief's heat. Don't default to the top levels.
- **茶餐廳 "Western" dishes:** a poor stand-in for European food, but in-culture for HK-style Western food (焗豬扒飯, 豉油皇炒麵, 奶茶, 西多士). Don't send notes that undo the sweetness or the evaporated milk.
- **Macanese restaurants:** a poor stand-in for a Portugal brief, but the home version of Macanese cuisine (minchi, African chicken, tacho).
- **Taiwanese 麻辣鍋 shops:** a poor stand-in for Chongqing or Chengdu hotpot (less numbing, sweeter, refillable 鴨血 and 豆腐), but in-culture for Taiwan's own 麻辣鍋.
- **Zhonghe 華新街 and Longgang shops:** a poor stand-in for a generic "Thai" or "Chinese" brief, but the best source for Burmese-Chinese and Yunnanese home food (mohinga, 米干, 擺夷 sour-spicy dishes).
- **Taiwanese 素食自助餐 (Buddhist vegetarian buffets):** a poor stand-in for Indian vegetarian food, but the real thing for Taiwanese Buddhist vegetarian cooking (mock meats, no 五辛).
- **Chungking Mansions curry houses:** often a weak stand-in for a regional Indian brief, but often the best in HK for Pakistani (karahi, nihari, haleem) and for Nepali or East African dishes.
- **Nepali-run "Indian" shops in Jordan:** order the Nepali menu (dal bhat, momo, sekuwa), the same flip as Japan and Korea.

## Hard-filter notes
- **Pork:** it is the default meat in both places. Expect it in lard (豬油) for frying and pastry, 肉燥 sauce over rice and noodles, bone broths, dumpling and bun fillings, 叉燒, XO sauce (often with ham) and many 茶餐廳 dishes. "Chicken" or "vegetable" dishes can still be cooked in lard or stock. For a no-pork filter, ask the shop, or use a halal-certified kitchen. **Pork-free is not halal.**
- **Vegetarian in Taiwan (素):** Taiwan's labeling rules for packaged food set five legal categories:
  - **全素 / 純素** (vegan)
  - **奶蛋素** (ovo-lacto; often said 蛋奶素)
  - **蛋素** (eggs, no dairy)
  - **奶素** (dairy, no eggs)
  - **植物五辛素** (vegetarian that allows the 五辛, and **may also contain milk or egg** if declared in the ingredients, so it is not vegan. The legal list is 蔥 scallion, 蒜 garlic, 韭 chives/leek, 蕎 rakkyo or shallot, and 興渠 asafoetida. In practice onion (洋蔥) is treated as 五辛 too)

  Buddhist 全素 also excludes the 五辛, so a 全素 dish is onion- and garlic-free. That suits Jain eaters except for root vegetables, so ask about those. Restaurants use the same words by custom but aren't held to the packaged-food rule. **鍋邊素** means vegetables cooked alongside meat or in the same pot or oil, with the meat picked out. It is not vegetarian for a strict brief. 蔬食 and 維根 are marketing words with no legal weight. TFDA announced a planned sixth category, provisionally "全植物素 VEGAN" (voluntary, for packaged food; scheduled to take effect 1 Jan 2028, so not in force yet and not a restaurant rule).
- **Vegetarian in HK and Macau:** 齋 / 素 restaurants are mostly Buddhist (no meat or fish, usually no 五辛). Egg and dairy vary, so ask. Oyster sauce and fish sauce hide in many "vegetable" dishes elsewhere.
- **Halal (Taiwan):** there is no government standard. Private bodies certify: the **Chinese Muslim Association (中國回教協會)**, which is linked to the Taipei Grand Mosque, and the **Taiwan Halal Integrity Development Association (THIDA)**, based at the Taipei Cultural Mosque. Other smaller certifiers exist. Informal "Muslim-friendly" marketing is not certification. There is also a formal Muslim Friendly Restaurant (MFR) certificate, issued by the Chinese Muslim Association and several private firms, which is weaker than full halal (MR); check the issuer and which outlet it covers. Mosque areas are useful search anchors: Taipei Grand Mosque (Da'an), Longgang Mosque (Zhongli), and mosques in Taichung, Tainan and Kaohsiung. Many Indonesian shops are Muslim-run but uncertified, so it is the eater's call.
- **Halal (HK and Macau):** the **Board of Trustees of the Islamic Community Fund of Hong Kong** issues halal certificates and keeps a "List of Halal Certified Restaurants in Hong Kong & Macau", also carried by the HK Tourism Board. The board describes itself as the only halal certifier in Hong Kong, and the Federation of Hong Kong Industries' Q-Mark halal scheme relies on its certificates; treat any other "halal" claim as uncertified. Mosques: Kowloon Mosque (Tsim Sha Tsui), Masjid Ammar in the Osman Ramju Sadick Islamic Centre (Wan Chai; its Islamic Centre Canteen serves Chinese-Muslim halal dim sum and dishes, reviewed as open in March 2026; 40 Oi Kwan Road), and the Jamia Mosque (Mid-Levels). Chungking Mansions is mostly community-halal, not certified.
- **Alcohol:** 米酒 (rice wine) and 紹興酒 are standard in Taiwanese and Cantonese cooking (三杯雞, 薑母鴨, 麻油雞, drunken chicken, many marinades). 麻油雞 and 薑母鴨 can be heavily alcoholic. Ask if this matters.
- **Labeling:** both places require allergen and ingredient labels on packaged food. Restaurant and delivery-app allergen info is inconsistent, so for a serious allergy confirm with the shop.

## Gaps
- **Taiwan:** Mexican (real nixtamal tortillas), Middle Eastern, Turkish, African and Latin American are rare. Regional Indian (South Indian, Bengali) is thin outside Taipei and Hsinchu. Korean, Japanese and Southeast Asian are strong.
- **Hong Kong:** coverage is broad. Latin American, Ethiopian and Sri Lankan are rare. Specialist kitchens in Chungking Mansions and on Temple Street upper floors may not be on either app, so plan for pickup.
- **Macau:** the scene is narrow outside Cantonese, Macanese, Portuguese and the casino-resort restaurants. The apps are Chinese-language, so expect to drive them in Chinese.
