# Market: United Kingdom (and Ireland, approximately)

> Ireland differs (derived, unverified): Dublin 'Indian' kitchens are mostly Indian-run (Punjabi, Keralan, South Indian) or Pakistani-run, not Sylheti; Parnell Street is the Asian quarter (Chinese, Korean); there is a large Brazilian community (Dublin, Gort) and Polish shops nationwide. Outside Dublin, Cork and Limerick have smaller Indian, Pakistani, Polish and Brazilian scenes.

## Local palate vector
The curry-house heat scale (korma → madras → vindaloo → phaal) works as a setting, not a dish. Expect sweet and creamy "safe" options, chips with everything, and a mild default with heat available on request.

The top of that scale (vindaloo, phaal) is a British curry-house invention, not home heat. Map the brief's heat to the dish's native level and pick the matching scale step. Pick the top step only when the brief's heat is at or above the dish's native top.

## Counter and line languages
English at the counter. Line languages: Sylheti Bengali in Bangladeshi curry houses (write notes in standard Bengali script বাংলা, never Syloti Nagri); Urdu and Punjabi / Mirpuri-Pothwari in Pakistani kitchens (Urdu in Nastaliq script); Nepali (Devanagari) in Nepali-run curry houses; Tamil in Sri Lankan / South Indian; Gujarati in Leicester/Wembley; Kurdish (Kurmanji) or Turkish in kebab shops; Cantonese, Mandarin or Fujianese in Chinese kitchens; often Polish, Romanian or Portuguese back-of-house in mainstream kitchens.

## Proxy flips
- Bangladeshi curry houses sometimes cook real Bangladeshi food (bhuna, shatkora, fish dishes) on a separate "Bangladeshi specials" section, or on request. They're a good bet for a Bengali brief. For home-style Bengali food, the Whitechapel / Shadwell cafés with bhorta and fish served cafeteria-style beat the Brick Lane curry strip.
- Nepali-run 'Indian & Nepalese' restaurants: order the Nepali section (momo, sekuwa, choila).
- 'Turkish' kebab shops are often Kurdish-run (from Turkey, Iraq or Iran): good for a Kurdish or Turkish grill brief, not for Turkish home cooking.
- Pakistani-run "Indian" grill houses (Bradford, Birmingham, Manchester Longsight): weak for Bengali or South Indian briefs, strong for a Punjabi/Pakistani brief (karahi, nihari, lamb chops, seekh).
- Sri Lankan Tamil kitchens under a "South Indian" sign: order the Sri Lankan section (kottu, hoppers, string hoppers, devilled dishes) for a Sri Lankan brief; they are not a Tamil Nadu or Keralan kitchen.
- Cantonese high-street takeaways: weak for the takeaway canon, but the Chinese-language menu or roast-meat counter can serve a Cantonese home brief (siu mei, congee, claypot rice).
- Pub Thai kitchens: Thai owner-cooks behind an anglicised menu; ask for the Thai-style or Isan dishes (som tam, larb) for a Thai brief.
- Provincial "kebab & pizza" shops run by Afghans or Iraqi/Iranian Kurds: sometimes cook kabuli palau or Kurdish rice and grills off the main menu; ask or check reviews.

## Hard-filter notes
- **Pork:** South Asian, Turkish/Kurdish, Arab, Afghan and Somali kitchens are almost always pork-free. Chinese, Filipino, Korean, Vietnamese, Polish and Portuguese kitchens use pork widely, including in stocks and fillings.
- **Halal:** there are two main certifiers. HMC (Halal Monitoring Committee) requires no pre-slaughter stunning; HFA (Halal Food Authority) accepts stunning. Many takeaways self-declare halal without a certificate. For a strict brief, check which body certifies, or ask.
- **Alcohol:** rare in South Asian cooking, but shaoxing wine and mirin are standard in Chinese and Japanese kitchens. Some curry houses are unlicensed and allow bring-your-own (irrelevant for delivery).
- **Labeling:** by law the 14 major allergens must be available on request, and platforms show a contact route for this. Hygiene ratings are public (FSA 0 to 5 in England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Pass / Improvement Required in Scotland) and make a useful filter. Ireland follows the same EU-derived 14-allergen rules.
