# Market: Canada

## Local palate vector
Close to the US (sweeter, milder by default), but with deeper in-culture immigrant food scenes in the big metros.

## Counter and line languages
English at the counter; French in Québec (the Charter of the French Language, strengthened by Bill 96, makes menus French-first, so write the counter line in French there). On the line: across Ontario, BC and the Prairies, kitchens of every cuisine are often staffed by Punjabi/Hindi speakers (many international students) or Filipino (Tagalog) cooks. They read English, so keep the counter line in plain English rather than guessing their language. In Montréal, line staff are often Arabic-, Haitian Kreyòl- or Spanish-speaking, so a plain French line is the safe default. Diaspora-owned kitchens where the cuisine's own language fits: Cantonese/Mandarin (Richmond, Markham), Tamil (Scarborough), Persian (North York), Punjabi (Brampton, Surrey).

## Platforms (as of 2026-11; re-verify after 6 months)
- **Uber Eats**: largest nationally. Field notes: `platforms/ubereats.md`.
- **DoorDash**: national.
- **SkipTheDishes**: Winnipeg-based, owned by Just Eat Takeaway, which Prosus took private in 2025. Often strongest in the Prairies (Winnipeg, Regina, Saskatoon, Calgary, Edmonton).
- **Fantuan (饭团外卖)** and **HungryPanda (熊猫外卖)**: check these for Chinese and wider Asian kitchens in Metro Vancouver and the GTA. Fantuan is based in Burnaby (Metro Vancouver) and bought Chowbus's delivery business in 2024. Many Richmond and Markham kitchens list their full Chinese menus only on these two apps.
- **Recent exits**: Foodora (Delivery Hero) left Canada in May 2020.

All are driven via `platforms/_browser.md`.

## Proxy flips
- 'Chinese' or 'Indian' places that list chilli chicken or Manchurian are often Hakka Indo-Chinese, in-culture for that tradition.
- Shawarma shops are often Lebanese-, Syrian- or Iraqi-run, so they're good for Levantine mezze or Iraqi tashreeb if listed.
- Sushi chains are often Korean- or Chinese-run, so look for Korean dishes on the same menu.
- Trini and Guyanese roti shops are Indo-Caribbean. They are a poor stand-in for Indian food but in-culture for doubles, curry duck, buss-up-shut and dhalpuri.
- Some pho shops (Toronto, Montréal, Vancouver) are run by ethnic-Chinese Vietnamese (Hoa) families. Check the menu for Teochew/Cantonese-Vietnamese dishes like hủ tiếu or mì.

## Hard-filter notes
- **Pork**: common and unrestricted. Chinese, Filipino, Korean and Portuguese kitchens use it widely, including in stocks and fillings, so ask about stock, not just the protein.
- **Halal**: no single national standard. The CFIA requires a halal claim on packaged food to name the certifying body. Common certifiers are HMA (stricter, hand-slaughter only) and ISNA Canada. Restaurant "halal" signs are often self-declared; check for a named certifier if it matters. Hubs: Mississauga, Thorncliffe Park, Scarborough, Montréal Park Extension, Edmonton, Calgary.
- **Kosher**: COR (Toronto) and MK (Montréal) are the main certifiers.
- **Alcohol**: rules are provincial. Several provinces allow alcohol with food delivery, with ID checked at the door. Wine and rice wine in Chinese cooking and beer marinades are normal and rarely listed.
- **Labeling**: restaurant menus are not required to list allergens or ingredients. For a serious allergy the user must confirm with the restaurant directly (a chef note is not confirmation), and treat Québec menu text as French-first.

## Gaps
Regional Mexican and Thai are thinner than in the US; outside Toronto, Vancouver and Montréal, regional depth drops sharply.
