# Market: South Korea

## Local palate vector
Sweet-spicy, garlic, gochujang. Foreign food gets Korean-ized: sweet potato or corn on pizza, sweeter sauces, extra cheese. Quick delivery is the norm.

## Counter and line languages
Korean. Foreign kitchens are often run by Nepali, Vietnamese, Chinese and ethnic-Korean Chinese (중국동포; avoid the label '조선족' in any note), Uzbek and other Russian-speaking Central Asian, Mongolian, or Thai staff. For Central Asian kitchens, Russian often works better than Uzbek as the note language.

## Platforms (as of 2026-11; re-verify after 6 months)
- **Baemin (배달의민족)**: the largest app, with the most foreign-cuisine coverage. Baemin's parent, Delivery Hero, agreed in July 2026 to be acquired by Uber (closing expected in the second half of 2027). Baemin says it stays operationally independent, and the app is unchanged for now.
- **Coupang Eats (쿠팡이츠)**: the clear #2. Coupang WOW members get free delivery.
- **땡겨요 (Ddangyo)**: Shinhan Bank's public app, with low fees and local-government discounts. It is growing fast and passed Yogiyo on weekly card spend in October 2025.
- **Yogiyo (요기요)**: now a minor player.
- **Recent exits:** Uber Eats left Korea in October 2019. Uber's return is through the Baemin deal, not a separate Uber Eats app.

Most apps need a Korean phone number to verify an account. Drive them via `platforms/_browser.md`.

## Proxy flips
- Nepali-run "Indian" kitchens: order momo, dal bhat and thukpa (the same flip as Japan).
- Joseonjok- or mainland-run 양꼬치 and 중국집 places: order Dongbei food (lamb skewers, 꿔바로우, 지삼선) rather than 한국식 중화 staples.
- Hwagyo-run old 중국집: order Shandong-rooted dishes (오향장육, 산둥식 만두, 고추잡채 with 꽃빵) rather than trendy Sichuan items.
- Uzbek- or Koryo-saram-run "Russian" places: order Central Asian dishes (plov, samsa, lagman, shashlik) and Koryo-saram dishes; Russian items (pelmeni, borscht) are usually also faithful.
- Mongolian kitchens around Dongdaemun: order buuz, khuushuur and tsuivan.
- Thai-run kitchens in industrial towns: order Isan food (som tam pla ra, larb, nam tok, sticky rice) rather than Bangkok-style pad thai.

## Hard-filter notes
- **Pork:** everywhere. Assume pork in 만두 fillings, jjajang sauce, kimchi-jjigae, many broths and much 분식, unless stated otherwise.
- **Halal:** certification comes from the Korea Muslim Federation (KMF). The Korea Tourism Organization also grades restaurants as Muslim-friendly (self-certified or Muslim-friendly levels), which is weaker than KMF. Halal hubs: Itaewon near the Seoul Central Mosque, Ansan, and Busan (Geumjeong-gu mosque area).
- **Alcohol:** 맛술 (mirin-style cooking wine) and soju are common in marinades and sauces, so ask if this matters.
- **Seafood and vegetarian:** kimchi usually contains 젓갈 (fermented seafood), and many soups use anchovy or beef stock. "Vegetable" dishes are often not vegetarian.
- **Labeling:** menus must state the country of origin (원산지) for meat, rice and kimchi. Allergen info is patchy outside large franchises.

## Gaps
West African, Ethiopian, Caribbean, Peruvian and Persian are rare.
