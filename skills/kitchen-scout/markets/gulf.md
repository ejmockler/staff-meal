# Market: Gulf (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

## Local palate vector
Rich, rice-and-meat, generally milder, with no pork and halal by default. International chains are adapted toward an Arab palate (more garlic sauce, less chili). When undoing this drift, aim for the brief's level, which defaults to what is usual for the dish at home. Do not overshoot to maximum heat or zero sugar.

## Counter and line languages
**Kitchen workforces are mostly South Asian (Malayali, Tamil, Hyderabadi and other Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Nepali, Sri Lankan) and Filipino.** Egyptian cooks are common in Arab and grill kitchens in Kuwait and Saudi Arabia, and Yemeni (often Hadrami) cooks run many mandi and madbi houses. A note in Malayalam, Urdu/Hindi, Bengali, Nepali or Tagalog may land better than an Arabic one, even at a non-South-Asian restaurant. Counter: English and Arabic in the UAE, Qatar and Bahrain. In Saudi Arabia, Saudization puts more Saudi nationals at the counter, so lead with Arabic there.

## Proxy flips
- Mid-market "international" kitchens are often staffed by Kerala or Filipino cooks. The staff meal can be better than the menu, and a note in their language can unlock it.
- South Asian cafeterias are a poor stand-in for Chinese but in-culture for Indo-Chinese (chilli chicken, Manchurian), and for Kerala parotta, beef fry and tea-shop snacks.
- Everyday "Arabic" rice restaurants are a poor stand-in for Khaleeji machboos or Najdi kabsa, but are often Yemeni-run: order their mandi or madbi instead.
- Generic "Indian" restaurants run by Pakistanis are a weak stand-in for North Indian but in-culture for Pakistani karahi, nihari and BBQ.
- Kuwaiti and Saudi grill and shawarma kitchens with Egyptian cooks are in-culture for Egyptian feteer, hawawshi and ful even when the sign says Lebanese.
- Filipino-run cafes and fast-food spots are a weak stand-in for American food but in-culture for silog breakfasts and pancit.

## Hard-filter notes
- **Pork:** effectively none in Saudi Arabia, Kuwait or Qatar (Qatar sells pork only at the QDC store, not in restaurants or hotels). In the UAE and Bahrain (and rarely Oman), some licensed hotel and non-halal restaurants serve pork, mainly in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Manama, but pork on delivery apps is rare. Check the listing and don't assume. At any halal kitchen, set substitutions in the brief up front (chicken or beef adobo, beef bacon, turkey ham).
- **Halal:** the default everywhere; meat is halal-certified at import or slaughter, so "halal" on a listing is rarely a differentiator. Non-halal outlets need separate licensing and label pork items.
- **Alcohol:** banned in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait; elsewhere served only at licensed venues. Delivery kitchens normally omit wine, mirin and sake or swap them, so expect dishes that depend on them to taste different and ask if it matters.
- **Labeling:** calorie counts are common on chain menus (required in Saudi Arabia). Allergen information is inconsistent, so confirm with the restaurant for serious allergies.

## Gaps
Pork dishes (see Hard-filter notes). Regional Chinese, Korean and Mexican outside Dubai are thin; widen the radius or accept the closest in-culture kitchen.
