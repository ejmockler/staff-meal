# Market: Australia and New Zealand

## Local palate vector
Moderately mild, but with strong Asian-food literacy, so drift is smaller than in the US or Japan. Sweet chili sauce shows up everywhere as the "safe" option. Dishes drift sweeter (Thai, Chinese), creamier (North Indian) and milder. Chef notes push the dish back to its home version. Heat follows the brief: ask for the app's top spice level only when the brief's heat is at or above the dish's native top.

## Counter and line languages
Counter: English. The line often isn't the cuisine's culture. International students (Nepali, Indian, Chinese, Colombian/Brazilian) and working-holiday makers (Korean, Japanese, Taiwanese) staff many kitchens, so confirm who is actually cooking (owner origin, reviews) before adding a language line. Large communities include Chinese, Vietnamese, Indian, Nepali, Sri Lankan, Lebanese, Greek, Italian, Afghan, Iraqi/Assyrian, Korean, Thai, Filipino, Indonesian and (in NZ especially) Pasifika.

## Platforms (as of 2026-11; re-verify after 6 months)
- **Australia:** Uber Eats (field notes: `platforms/ubereats.md`) and DoorDash. Recent exits: Menulog stopped taking orders on 26 Nov 2025, under a deal that moved its customers and restaurants to Uber Eats. Deliveroo left in 2022.
- **New Zealand:** Uber Eats, DoorDash and Delivereasy. Delivereasy is NZ-owned and strong in regional towns (Dunedin, Tauranga, Wairarapa) and with independents, so check it when an independent isn't on Uber. Recent exits: Menulog left NZ in May 2024.

All are driven via `platforms/_browser.md`.

## Proxy flips
- "Indian" places run by Nepali students may do excellent momo and dal bhat. The same logic as Japan applies, at a smaller scale.
- "Indian" places run by Sri Lankan Tamils: weaker for North Indian curry, often good for kottu, hoppers and Sri Lankan curries.
- "Chinese" places run by Malaysian-Chinese owners: often better for char kway teow, laksa and Hainanese chicken rice than for regional Chinese food.
- Sushi takeaways run by Korean owners (common): weak as Japanese, but may do good Korean dishes (bibimbap, kimchi jjigae) when they are on the menu.
- "Thai" places with Isan owners: better for som tam, larb and grilled meats than for central Thai curries.
- "Kebab" or charcoal-chicken shops run by Afghan owners (Dandenong and elsewhere): check for Afghan dishes such as kabuli palaw and bolani.

## Hard-filter notes
- **Pork:** widely available. Lebanese, Turkish, Afghan and other Muslim-run kitchens usually serve none.
- **Halal:** certified by private bodies (several in Australia; FIANZ in NZ), not the government. A "halal" tag on a delivery app is often self-declared, so check the shop's own page or reviews for a certificate. Halal kitchens cluster in Lakemba, Auburn and Dandenong. Halal Chinese (Uyghur, Hui) restaurants exist in Sydney and Melbourne.
- **Alcohol:** restaurants need a liquor licence to deliver alcohol, and drivers check ID. Wine or mirin in sauces is not flagged on menus, so ask.
- **Labeling:** restaurants don't have to list allergens on menus, but must give allergen information when asked. Some states require kilojoule counts on chain menus.

## Gaps
Regional Mexican, Caribbean and West African are thin. Ethiopian is small but present in Melbourne (Footscray).
