## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these; ask. Pork is the default meat in much of the cuisine, and fish sauce is in almost everything.
- **Pork-free is not halal.** Pork (heo in the South, lợn in the North) hides in broths (bún bò Huế uses pork hock and bones), chả/giò lụa sausage, pâté, xíu mại and mỡ hành scallion oil (often pork fat). A "no pork" request does not make the beef or chicken halal. Ask: "Món này có thịt heo hoặc mỡ heo không ạ?" (Does this dish have pork or pork fat?)
- **Lard and pork fat in non-pork dishes**: mỡ hành on cơm tấm, bánh hỏi, bánh bèo; tóp mỡ (pork crackling) on mì Quảng and cơm hến. Ask: "Cho mình xin không mỡ hành, không tóp mỡ ạ."
- **Fish sauce and shrimp paste in "vegetarian" food**: nước mắm, mắm tôm (with bún đậu, bún riêu), mắm ruốc (bún bò Huế). Only "chay" (Buddhist vegetarian) food is reliably free of them. Strict chay also omits onion, garlic and scallion. Ask: "Mình ăn chay, cho mình xin không nước mắm, không mắm tôm, không mỡ heo ạ." Add "không hành tỏi ạ" for strict chay.
- **Meats many eaters don't expect**: huyết (congealed pig blood, standard in bún bò Huế and some bún riêu), chả cua (crab cake, in bún bò Huế), lòng (offal), gân (tendon) and sách (tripe) in phở, sụn (cartilage), ốc (snails), trứng vịt lộn (fertilized duck egg). Gloss them before ordering. Ask to leave one out: "Cho mình xin không huyết ạ."
- **Peanuts** (đậu phộng in the South, lạc in the North) top many noodle dishes and sauces, such as mì Quảng and the gỏi cuốn dip. Ask: "Cho mình xin không đậu phộng ạ."
- **Buddhist fasting**: many people eat chay on the 1st and 15th of the lunar month (mùng một, rằm). An eater who is "eating chay today" needs the full chay request above.

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Phở**: one name, two dishes. Name which. Northern home = clear broth with scallion; the table has lime, chili and garlic vinegar, with no herb plate, sprouts, hoisin or sriracha. Southern and export = bean sprouts, Thai basil, hoisin and sriracha on the side.
- **Bún bò Huế**: home = noticeably spicy (heat 5), with mắm ruốc depth, lemongrass and a chili-oil sheen. Export = tamed toward heat 1–2, with the shrimp paste cut. Ask for it the way it is made in Huế, at native level. Go hotter only on the eater's word.
- **Sweetness**: Southern home cooking is moderately sweet (nước chấm, kho, cơm tấm marinade), and Northern is lightly sweet. Abroad, both get sweeter. Ask for "ít ngọt" (less sweet), not no sugar. Ask for "không đường" only for dishes that have no sugar at home.
- **Herbs**: Southern-style dishes, bún chả, bánh xèo and rolls need a full plate of rau sống/rau thơm (tía tô perilla, rau răm, húng lủi mint, ngò gai culantro, húng quế Thai basil, kinh giới), not one sprig of mint. Northern phở has no herb plate (see above).

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Kitchens cooking for outsiders sweeten dipping sauces and stir-fries, cut fish sauce and shrimp paste, tame bún bò Huế, merge Northern and Southern phở into the Southern style, and shrink the herb plate.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** Vietnamese, written in Quốc ngữ (Latin script) with full diacritics, since text without them is ambiguous. There is no separate romanization. Northern and Southern words differ: heo/lợn (pork), bột ngọt/mì chính (MSG), đậu phộng/lạc (peanut), ngò/mùi (coriander). Use the word that matches the kitchen's region, and either one is understood. Some hủ tiếu and noodle shops are run by Hoa (Chinese-Vietnamese) families; Vietnamese is still the right language there. Fallback: English plus dish names.

**Address default:** address the shop (quán), not a person. Refer to yourself as mình (neutral) and end requests with ạ. Use anh or chị only when the cook's gender is known (the paired "anh chị" is fine as an inclusive form). Never call the cook em, which marks them as junior.

## Meal composition norms
One noodle bowl per person is a full meal. For rice meals, order a shared main, a soup (canh) and a vegetable, with rice per person. Fish sauce dip, chili and fresh herbs are always on the table.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- A Vietnamese-language menu and regional dishes beyond phở and spring rolls (bún bò Huế, bún chả, cơm tấm).
- Hanoi-style phở is a specialist signal.
