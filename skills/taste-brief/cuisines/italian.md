# Italian: profile

Heat is mostly irrelevant here (except in Calabria). Fidelity is about **technique and restraint**: what's left out matters as much as what's in.

## Traditions
| Tradition | Signature dishes | Flavor signature | Native defaults | Staple carb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rome / Lazio | carbonara, cacio e pepe, amatriciana, gricia, supplì, pizza tonda | guanciale, pecorino, black pepper | heat 1 (side: none) · sweet none · sour low · rich high | pasta, thin crisp pizza |
| Naples / Campania | pizza napoletana, ragù napoletano, genovese, pasta e patate | San Marzano tomato, fior di latte, buffalo mozzarella | heat 0 (side: olio piccante) · sweet low · sour low · rich med | soft pizza, pasta |
| Bologna / Emilia | tagliatelle al ragù, tortellini in brodo, lasagne verdi, mortadella | ragù, Parmigiano, egg pasta | heat 0 (side: none) · sweet none · sour none · rich high | egg pasta |
| Sicily | arancine, pasta alla Norma, sarde, caponata, cannoli | sweet-sour (agrodolce), eggplant, citrus | heat 0 (side: peperoncino) · sweet med · sour med · rich med | pasta, bread |
| Liguria | pesto genovese, focaccia, trofie | basil, pine nut, olive oil | heat 0 (side: none) · sweet none · sour none · rich med | trofie, focaccia |
| Calabria | 'nduja, fileja alla 'nduja, soppressata, olio santo | peperoncino, pork | heat 4 (side: olio santo, dried peperoncino) · sweet none · sour low · rich high | pasta, bread |
| Italian-American | chicken parm, fettuccine alfredo, garlic bread, meatballs with spaghetti, red sauce | garlic, oregano, cheese-heavy | heat 0 (side: crushed red pepper) · sweet low · sour low · rich high | spaghetti, bread |

Sweet "med" in Sicily is the agrodolce of caponata and sarde; desserts (cannoli) are sweet anywhere. Calabrian dishes without 'nduja or peperoncino sit nearer heat 1.

## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these; ask. Italian kitchens rarely label them.
- **Pork is everywhere, often hidden.** Guanciale, pancetta, salsiccia, mortadella, 'nduja and soppressata are pork. **Strutto** (lard) can be in piadina, taralli, sfogliatelle and some breads and focaccia. Tortellini filling is pork-based. Pork-free ≠ halal: halal also needs the meat source checked, and wine (sfumato) goes into many sauces and risotti.
- **"Vegetarian" traps.** Risotto and soups are often made with **brodo** (meat or chicken stock). Parmigiano Reggiano and Grana Padano are made with animal rennet, and so is most pecorino. Fresh egg pasta is not vegan.
- **Anchovy.** Acciughe/alici are in puttanesca, salsa verde, bagna cauda, some pizzas and many broccoli/cime di rapa dishes. **Colatura di alici** is a Campanian fish sauce.
- **Meats many eaters don't expect (gloss them):** trippa (tripe), lampredotto (cow's fourth stomach, Florence), pajata (milk-fed veal intestine, Rome), coda alla vaccinara (oxtail), coniglio (rabbit), cavallo/asino (horse/donkey, in parts of Puglia and Veneto).
- **Fasting:** observant Catholics may keep meatless Fridays in Lent (*di magro*); fish is allowed.

| Ask | Italian (polite request) |
|---|---|
| Any pork, including lard or stock? | Scusate, questo piatto contiene carne di maiale, anche strutto, pancetta o brodo? |
| Fully vegetarian, no meat stock? | È completamente vegetariano, senza carne e senza brodo di carne? |
| Any anchovy or colatura? | Contiene acciughe o colatura di alici? |
| Any wine in the sauce? | C'è vino nel sugo o nella cottura? |
| Cheese with animal rennet? | Il formaggio è fatto con caglio animale? |

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Carbonara**: home = guanciale, egg yolk, pecorino and black pepper, emulsified; no cream, no garlic. Export = often cream-based, with bacon.
- **Pasta with chicken**: home = chicken is not a pasta topping (it's a secondo). Export = chicken alfredo, chicken penne.
- **Seafood pasta**: home = no cheese on vongole or frutti di mare. Ask "senza formaggio".
- **Pesto alla genovese**: home = with trofie or trenette, often with potato and green beans. Export = any pasta, sometimes with cream.
- **Pizza napoletana**: home = soft, with a wet center and a puffy, leopard-spotted cornicione; eaten with knife and fork, or folded 'a portafoglio'. If you want crisp, order a Roman-style pizza (tonda romana / scrocchiarella), don't ask for a Neapolitan one 'ben cotta'.
- **Ragù bolognese**: home = meaty with little tomato, served with tagliatelle. Export = tomato-heavy, on spaghetti.
- **Tomato sauce**: home = not sweetened in most regions; acidity is balanced by long cooking or a pinch at most. Italian-American red sauce is often sweeter; ask "less sweet" for that tradition, "no sugar" for an Italian one.
- **Agrodolce (caponata, sarde)**: home = sweet-sour by design. If the eater wants it toned down, ask for less sweet, not none.
- **Heat**: the home plate is mild almost everywhere; chili comes from the table (olio piccante, peperoncino). Raise heat only when the eater asks.
- **Italian-American, Japanese yōshoku (Napolitan) and wafū pasta (mentaiko, soy-butter)** are legitimate cuisines of their own. Just don't call them Italian on an Italian brief.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Kitchens cooking for outsiders tend to add cream and garlic, use bacon for guanciale, overcook pasta, sweeten tomato sauce, pile on cheese (including on seafood), and add chicken to pasta.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** Italian-run kitchens: standard Italian. Regional languages (Neapolitan, Sicilian and others) are spoken by many cooks, but write notes in standard Italian, which every Italian cook reads; don't attempt dialect. Italian-American kitchens: English. Fallback: English plus Italian dish names.

**Address default:** a neutral greeting ("Buonasera!") or "Chef". "Ciao ragazzi" is warm and common but casual, and literally "guys/kids"; use it only if the kitchen's own tone is that informal.

## Meal composition norms
The Italian sequence is antipasto → primo (pasta) → secondo (protein) + contorno (vegetable). For delivery, a sensible version is one pasta per person plus a shared antipasto or contorno. Pizza is one per person, uncut in Naples.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- Italian-only regional menus and guanciale on the ingredient list.
- AVPN (Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana) membership, or the 'STG' pizza napoletana mark, for Neapolitan pizza.
- Italian-American red-sauce joints are their own tradition. Tell them apart from Italian-run kitchens.
