**Native defaults** = the plate as cooked at home. In most Mexican food the dish itself is mild to moderate; heat comes from salsa each eater adds at the table, so it is listed under `side`.

## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these. Ask in Spanish; confirm in the menu description when possible.
- **Pork is everywhere, often unnamed:** al pastor, carnitas, cochinita, chicharrón, buche, longaniza/chorizo (often in campechanos), relleno negro (turkey plus pork). Pozole is usually pork. Ask: "¿Lleva carne de cerdo o manteca de cerdo?"
- **Lard (manteca) in "vegetarian" food:** refried beans, tamales, flour tortillas, tlayudas (asiento is pork lard), some masa. Ask: "Es para una persona vegetariana: ¿los frijoles o la masa llevan manteca?"
- **Chicken broth in "veg" sides:** arroz rojo and many moles are cooked with caldo de pollo. Ask: "¿El arroz o el mole lleva caldo de pollo?"
- **Pork-free ≠ halal.** Beef, goat and chicken are almost never halal-slaughtered; filter at kitchen-scout, not in a note. If asking: "¿La carne es halal?"
- **Meats many eaters don't expect (gloss them):** suadero (beef brisket/navel), tripa (intestine), lengua (tongue), cabeza (head meat), sesos (brain), buche (pork stomach), moronga (blood sausage), chapulines (grasshoppers), escamoles (ant larvae). Birria is goat in Jalisco, beef in Tijuana style; name which.
- **Seafood:** dried shrimp in Lent dishes (tortitas de camarón) and some caldos. Ask: "¿Lleva camarón o mariscos?"
- **Dairy:** crema, quesillo, queso fresco, cotija. Ask: "Sin queso ni crema, por favor."
- **Lent (Cuaresma):** meatless Fridays are a custom, not a rule; seafood and capirotada appear on menus then.

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Street tacos** (central/south): home = corn tortillas, cilantro, onion, lime, salsa on the side; export adds yellow cheese, sour cream, lettuce. Ask for the home version.
- **Salsa is where the heat lives.** Home = mild taco, the eater spoons on salsa. Ask for the house salsas on the side so the eater dials heat; ask for a hotter salsa only on the eater's word.
- **Mole**: home = traditional sweetness (chocolate, fruit, some sugar) that belongs, sweet med. Export versions can be candy-sweet; then ask "que no quede tan dulce, como en casa". Never "sin azúcar" for mole.
- **Chiles en nogada**: home = sweet-savory fruit picadillo and walnut sauce, mild. Keep it; don't ask for heat or no sugar.
- **Rice and beans on every plate** is a Tex-Mex combo convention, not a rule elsewhere.
- **Birria**: Jalisco = goat, often served dry or in its broth; Tijuana style = beef with consomé for dipping. Name which.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Kitchens cooking for outsiders add yellow cheese, sour cream and lettuce; swap corn for flour tortillas; sweeten mole and salsas; drop the table salsas or make one mild salsa; bundle rice and beans with everything.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** write notes in Mexican Spanish (chile, picoso, salsa; not Spain-Spanish terms). Cooks from Oaxaca or Yucatán may speak Zapotec, Mixtec or Maya at home, but kitchen Spanish is the right note language; don't attempt those languages. Tex-Mex kitchens: English plus Spanish dish names. Fallback: English plus dish names.

**Address default:** "Hola, equipo de cocina." Avoid "Jefe/Jefa" (gendered) and "Joven" (marks the cook as junior) in notes. Use usted forms.

## Meal composition norms
3–5 small tacos per person. Each main needs salsa, onion-cilantro and lime. Antojitos are a meal on their own, not a side.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- Regional specifics: cochinita, tlayuda, birria de chivo (Jalisco) or birria de res con consomé (Tijuana style).
- Corn tortillas made from nixtamal.
- Several table salsas, not one.
- Spanish-language reviews and menu board.
