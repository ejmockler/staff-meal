## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these. Ask, and confirm in the cook's language.
- **Pork**: black pork curry and Burgher lamprais (the traditional mixed-meat curry includes pork) are pork. "Devilled" and "mixed" kottu can be pork. **Pork-free is not halal.** Sri Lankan Muslim (Moor, Malay) kitchens are usually halal; others are not by default.
- **Beef**: many Sinhala Buddhists and most Tamil Hindus avoid beef. Devilled beef, beef kottu and some lamprais contain it.
- **Maldive fish (umbalakada / maasi)** goes into pol sambol, seeni sambol and many "vegetable" curries. A dish labeled vegetable is not vegetarian until confirmed.
- **Shrimp paste**: blachan in lamprais.
- **Ghee**: lamprais rice, some thosai and sweets.
- **Egg**: egg hoppers, egg kottu, lamprais egg.
- **Meats glossed**: ura mas = pork; harak mas = beef; elu mas / "mutton" = goat (in Sri Lanka "mutton" usually means goat); kukul mas = chicken; isso = prawn; dallo = cuttlefish.
- **Fasting**: some Sinhala Buddhists eat vegetarian on Poya (full-moon) days. Tamil Hindu fasting (viratham) is vegetarian, often without egg. Muslim kitchens observe Ramadan hours.

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Black pork curry**: home = dark-roasted curry powder and goraka, dark and tangy, heat about 6–7. Export = brown and generic, milder. Restore the dark roast and the sourness, not extra heat.
- **Kottu**: home = chopped and clattered on the griddle with curry gravy mixed in, heat about 6. Export = drier and milder. Ask for it as normally made.
- **Hoppers (appa / appam)**: home = bowl-shaped fermented rice-coconut pancake, lacy crisp edge, soft center; an egg hopper (bittara appa) has an egg cooked in the bowl. The hopper itself is not spicy; heat comes from lunu miris, seeni sambol and/or pol sambol, plus a curry. **String hoppers (indi appa / idiyappam)** come with kiri hodi (coconut-milk gravy), pol sambol and a curry. Kerala appam is a related but different preparation; name which.
- **Seeni sambol**: home = caramelized onion relish, sweet and hot. If the eater wants it less sweet, ask "less sugar", never "no sugar".
- **Devilled dishes**: home = sweet-sour-chili with tomato sauce, heat about 5. Ask "less sweet" only on the eater's word.
- **Heat**: home curries sit around 6–7 (Jaffna curries 7–8). Export kitchens often drop them to 3–4. Restore the native level; go higher only on the eater's word.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Curries made milder and creamier, curry powder un-roasted, goraka and tamarind cut back, sambols missing or replaced with chutney. Menus may file Sri Lankan dishes under "South Indian".

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** pick Sinhala or Tamil from the kitchen's provenance, never one for the other. Sinhala/Tamil is a sensitive pair. Sinhala-run kitchens get Sinhala plus English. Jaffna, Tamil-run and most Muslim (Moor) kitchens get Tamil. Sri Lankan Tamil endings (-ungo) differ from Indian Tamil (-unga); match the cook. Sinhala phrasing is less verified than the Tamil.

**Address default:** no address term, or "Hi Chef / kitchen team". Use aiyā (අයියා) / akkā (අක්කා) or annai (அண்ணை, Jaffna) / anna (அண்ணா) / akka (அக்கா) only when the cook's gender is known. Never "malli" or "thambi" (marks the cook as junior).

## Meal composition norms
Rice & curry is rice plus 3–5 curries (meat, dal parippu, a vegetable, mallum greens) and a sambol. Hoppers: 3–4 per person, plus 1 egg hopper. Kottu is a full meal.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- Hoppers, kottu, black pork curry or lamprais on the menu, and pol sambol served as standard.
- Sinhala- or Tamil-language reviews.
- A kitchen labeled "South Indian" can be Sri Lankan Tamil-run. Check for Sri Lankan dishes.
