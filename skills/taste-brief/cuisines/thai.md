## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these from the cuisine label. Ask, with the user's particle (ครับ/ค่ะ in statements, ครับ/คะ in questions).
- **Pork-free is not halal.** Pork (หมู, mu) and lard (น้ำมันหมู, namman mu) are common in Central and Northern cooking. A kitchen that leaves out pork is not halal. Southern Thai-Muslim kitchens are often halal; confirm: ร้านนี้ฮาลาลไหมครับ/คะ (ran ni halan mai khrap/kha, "Is this restaurant halal?"). No pork: ไม่ใส่หมูครับ/ค่ะ (mai sai mu khrap/kha).
- **Fish sauce and shrimp paste are in almost everything.** Curry pastes contain shrimp paste (กะปิ, kapi); Isan salads use pla ra (ปลาร้า). "Vegetarian" (มังสวิรัติ, mangsawirat) may still include them. Ask: ไม่ใส่น้ำปลา กะปิ และปลาร้าครับ/ค่ะ (mai sai nam pla, kapi lae pla ra khrap/kha). Oyster sauce (น้ำมันหอย, namman hoi) shows up in "vegetable" stir-fries.
- **เจ (je) is stricter than vegetarian.** Chinese-Thai Buddhist food: no meat, fish, egg or dairy, and also no garlic, onion, shallot or chives. Kitchens run it during the Vegetarian Festival (9th lunar month). Ask: ขอแบบเจครับ/ค่ะ (kho baep je khrap/kha).
- **Blood and raw meat.** Boat noodles (ก๋วยเตี๋ยวเรือ) can be thickened with blood; Northern larb lueat and some larb dip use raw meat or raw blood. Ask: มีเลือดไหมครับ/คะ (mi lueat mai khrap/kha, "Is there blood in it?"); for raw dishes: ขอสุกครับ/ค่ะ (kho suk khrap/kha, "Cooked through, please").
- **Raw pickled crab.** Som tam pu / pu pla ra uses salted raw field crab. Flag it for anyone avoiding raw shellfish.
- **Unexpected meats, glossed:** khrueang nai (เครื่องใน, offal), sai krok/sai ua (pork sausage), khai mot daeng (red ant eggs), insects (แมลงทอด) at Isan kitchens.
- **Peanuts:** pad thai, som tam Thai, massaman, satay sauce. Ask: ไม่ใส่ถั่วลิสงครับ/ค่ะ (mai sai thua lisong khrap/kha).
- **Fasting:** Thai-Muslim kitchens may change hours during Ramadan.

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Sweetness.** Home: pad thai, pad see ew and green curry are mildly sweet; kaphrao, som tam Lao/pla ra, larb, nam tok and southern curries are not sweet. Export kitchens sweeten everything. For dishes sweet at home, ask for หวานน้อย (waan noi, less sweet), not ไม่หวาน. Keep ไม่หวาน (mai waan) for dishes that shouldn't taste sweet. Pad thai at home comes with the condiment set (khrueang prung: chili flakes, sugar, prik nam pla, vinegar chili), so the eater adjusts it.
- **Pad kaphrao**: home = holy basil (ใบกะเพรา, not horapha), crushed garlic and bird's-eye chili, no filler vegetables, fried egg on top; export = sweet basil, onion, carrot, peppers. Order: กะเพราหมูสับไข่ดาว (kaphrao mu sap khai dao) + ไม่ใส่หอมใหญ่ แครอท หรือผักอื่น (no onion, carrot or other veg). Optional: ไข่ดาวไม่สุก (runny yolk) or ไข่ดาวกรอบ (crispy edges).
- **Som tam**: one name, two dishes. "Som tam Thai" is the sweeter version with dried shrimp and peanuts. "Som tam Lao / pla ra / pu pla ra" is the funky Isan version. Name which.
- **Heat.** Home heat is the level in the Traditions table, and the eater adds more from the condiment set. "Thai hot" means very different things to a Thai cook and to an export kitchen. Use the Thai phrase for the normal Thai level, not the English one. Ask for more heat only on the eater's word.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Sweeter sauces, milder curries, extra coconut cream, filler vegetables in stir-fries, sweet basil for holy basil, jasmine rice served with Isan and Northern food instead of sticky rice.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** write in Thai for most kitchens, including Northern (spoken Kham Mueang, written Thai) and Southern ones. Lao/Thai is a sensitive pair: if the kitchen is Lao-run, write in Lao, not Thai, even for som tam and larb. Isan cooks read Thai. Fallback: English plus dish names in Thai script.

**Particles:** ครับ (khrap) / ค่ะ (kha) follow the USER's gender, not the cook's. Ask the user, and put the same particle on every request line. Questions take คะ (kha, high tone) instead of ค่ะ. If the user's gender is unknown, use the row's neutral form where given.

**Address default:** no address term. คุณ (khun) is the polite neutral option. พี่ (phi, older sibling) is warm but familial; use it only when the user wants that register. Never น้อง (nong), which marks the cook as junior.

## Meal composition norms
Two modes. One-plate dishes (อาหารจานเดียว: kaphrao, khao pad, khao man gai, noodles) are one per person. Shared spreads (กับข้าว) are one dish per person plus one, with rice for each person: sticky rice for Isan or Northern food, jasmine for Central and Southern. Pair fiery dishes with fresh raw vegetables (ผักสด) or herbs.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic)
Thai-run kitchens often keep a separate Thai-language menu or a "Thai-style" option. Thai-language reviews and Isan-specific dishes (pla ra som tam, nam tok) are strong tells. Market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`.
