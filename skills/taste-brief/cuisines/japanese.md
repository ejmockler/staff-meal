# Japanese: profile

Heat is the wrong axis here. Japanese fidelity is about **seasoning strength, texture, temperature and freshness**. The brief should capture those instead of heat. Most home dishes are heat 0–2; heat lives in table condiments (shichimi, karashi, wasabi, yuzu kosho, rāyu).

## Traditions (never treat the country as one cuisine)
| Tradition | Signature dishes | Flavor signature | Native defaults | Staple carb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Edo-mae / Tokyo | nigiri sushi, soba, tempura, monja | sharper soy, dark dashi | heat 0 (side: wasabi, shichimi) · sweet low · sour low · rich low | rice, soba |
| Kansai (Osaka/Kyoto) | Osaka-style okonomiyaki, takoyaki, kitsune udon, kushikatsu, Kyoto kaiseki/obanzai | usukuchi soy (pale but saltier), kombu-forward dashi | heat 0 (side: shichimi, karashi) · sweet med · sour low · rich med | udon, rice |
| Kyushu | Hakata tonkotsu ramen, karashi mentaiko, chicken nanban, motsunabe, Nagasaki champon, toriten | rich pork bone, sweet Kyushu soy, yuzu kosho | heat 1 (side: karashi takana, yuzu kosho) · sweet med · sour low · rich high | thin noodles, rice |
| Hokkaido | miso ramen (Sapporo), soup curry, jingisukan, seafood bowls | butter, corn, miso | heat 1 (side: soup curry heat chosen per order) · sweet low · sour none · rich high | ramen, rice |

| Yoshoku (Japanese-Western) | omurice, hamburg steak, Japanese curry, Napolitan | ketchup, demi-glace, roux | heat 1 (curry at chūkara ≈ 2) · sweet med · sour low · rich high | rice |

## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these; ask. Phrases are polite Japanese.
- **Pork is everywhere it isn't named**: tonkotsu broth, chāshū, gyoza filling, katsu, lard in ramen tare and fried rice. Ask: 豚肉や豚由来の材料(ラード、豚骨スープ)は入っていますか? (Butaniku ya buta-yurai no zairyō (rādo, tonkotsu sūpu) wa haitte imasu ka?)
- **Pork-free ≠ halal.** Mirin and sake (alcohol) season teriyaki, sukiyaki, nimono and many tare; meat is rarely halal-slaughtered. Ask: ハラール対応はできますか? (Harāru taiō wa dekimasu ka?) and みりんやお酒は使っていますか? (Mirin ya o-sake wa tsukatte imasu ka?)
- **"Vegetarian" with fish dashi**: miso soup, udon/soba broth, agedashi tofu and nimono usually use katsuobushi (bonito). Shōjin ryōri (Buddhist temple food) uses kombu/shiitake dashi instead. Ask: 魚のだし(かつお節)は使っていますか? (Sakana no dashi (katsuobushi) wa tsukatte imasu ka?)
- **Buckwheat allergy (soba) can be severe**, and shops often boil udon in the same water as soba. Ask: そばアレルギーがあります。そば粉なしで、茹で釜も別でお願いできますか? (Soba arerugī ga arimasu. Sobako nashi de, yudegama mo betsu de onegai dekimasu ka?)
- **Wheat in soy sauce**: standard shōyu contains wheat; gluten-free needs tamari, and the kitchen must confirm.
- **Raw egg** (sukiyaki dip, tsukimi, tamago kake gohan) and **raw or seared-rare chicken** (torisashi, tataki). Ask: 生卵なしでお願いします (Nama tamago nashi de onegaishimasu).
- **Unexpected meats (gloss them)**: basashi (raw horse), motsu/horumon (offal), shirako (cod milt), kujira (whale, rare), uni (sea urchin roe).

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Sushi**: in Japan it's nigiri with wasabi already under the fish (サビ入り), rice at body temperature, and lightly vinegared. Western-style uramaki (cream cheese, spicy-mayo drizzle, tempura flakes) is a separate diaspora tradition. Japan has its own rolls (futomaki, hosomaki) and mayo gunkan at kaiten shops, so "no mayo" is a preference, not a fidelity rule.
- **Teriyaki**: home = a thin, glossy soy-mirin-sugar tare, sweet-savory and lightly applied; export = a thick, sweeter glaze. Ask for the thin tare, or "less sweet", never "no sugar".
- **Okonomiyaki**: one name, two preparations. Osaka style = batter mixed with the fillings; Hiroshima style = layered, with yakisoba/udon noodles. Name which.
- **Ramen**: noodle firmness is ordered (かため katame / ばりかた barikata for Hakata), along with seasoning strength (濃いめ koime / 薄め usume) and fat level (油多め abura ōme / 少なめ sukuname).
- **Japanese curry**: home = a roux curry, savory with a sweet undertone, usually 中辛 chūkara (medium, about heat 2). Shops sell levels: 甘口 amakuchi (mild/sweet), 中辛 chūkara, 辛口 karakuchi (hot), and numbered levels at curry chains (e.g. CoCo壱番屋 1–10辛). Default to the shop's normal level; go up the shop's own scale only on the eater's word. Don't ask a curry shop to make it Indian.
- **Rice**: short-grain, glossy and not dry.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Sauces get sweeter and heavier (eel sauce and spicy-mayo drizzles, thick teriyaki), rolls replace nigiri, cream cheese and deep-fried rolls appear, rice is served cold or over-vinegared, horseradish paste stands in for wasabi, and menus mix in pan-Asian dishes.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** Japanese kitchens take standard polite Japanese; Okinawan or other dialects aren't needed for ordering. Many "Japanese" kitchens abroad are run by cooks of other backgrounds: pick the note language from the kitchen's provenance, and fall back to English plus Japanese dish names.

**Address default:** Japanese notes don't address the cook. Open with the request (〜でお願いします) and close with よろしくお願いします. Don't write 大将 (counter-regular slang, male-coded).

| Intent | Language | Native phrase | Script | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as it's normally made at home (native level) | Japanese | Honba no aji de onegaishimasu. / Nihon de taberu yō na ajitsuke de onegaishimasu. | 本場の味でお願いします。/ 日本で食べるような味付けでお願いします。 | |
| a bit hotter than usual (only on the eater's word) | Japanese | Sukoshi karame ni shite itadakemasu ka? | 少し辛めにしていただけますか? | For curry, name one step up the shop's own scale instead. |
| less sweet (dishes sweet at home) | Japanese | Amasa hikaeme de onegaishimasu. | 甘さ控えめでお願いします。 | Teriyaki, sukiyaki, gyudon, unagi and nimono are sweet in Japan too; ask for less, not none. |
| no sugar (dishes not sweet at home) | Japanese | Satō nashi de onegaishimasu. | 砂糖なしでお願いします。 | Rarely right: most home tare and simmered dishes use sugar or mirin. Use for drinks, or a dish the kitchen sweetened beyond home use. |
| dry / no gravy | Japanese | Tsuyu sukuname de onegaishimasu. / Tare wa betsu-zoe de onegaishimasu. | つゆ少なめでお願いします。/ タレは別添えでお願いします。 | Less broth on a donburi / sauce on the side. |
| firm noodles | Japanese | Men katame de onegaishimasu. | 麺かためでお願いします。 | |
| stronger / lighter seasoning | Japanese | Aji koime de onegaishimasu. / Aji usume de onegaishimasu. | 味濃いめでお願いします。/ 味薄めでお願いします。 | |
| wasabi in the sushi | Japanese | Wasabi-iri de onegaishimasu. | わさび入りでお願いします。 | |
| no mayonnaise | Japanese | Mayonēzu nashi de onegaishimasu. | マヨネーズなしでお願いします。 | |
| polite opener (neutral) | Japanese | (none; open with the request) … Yoroshiku onegaishimasu. | …よろしくお願いします。 | Closing line, not an opener. No address term. |
| thanks | Japanese | Arigatō gozaimasu. | ありがとうございます。 | |

Japanese notes should be polite and indirect (〜でお願いします / 〜していただけると嬉しいです). Demands in a blunt register backfire.

## Meal composition norms
A set (定食 teishoku) is the main, rice, miso soup and pickles. Order a la carte for izakaya-style sharing. For ramen, one bowl per person plus gyoza or rice to share.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- Japanese-language reviews, an omakase or nigiri focus, seasonal fish listed by Japanese name, and real wasabi.
- A branch of a Japanese ramen chain (Ichiran, Ippudo, Afuri) is usually close to spec on its core bowls. Check that the core menu (e.g. Ippudo Shiromaru/Akamaru, Ichiran's custom-order sheet) is offered; localized add-ons are not a fidelity signal.
