# Bangladeshi: profile

Bangladeshi food is not "Indian", and it is not the same thing as West Bengal (Kolkata) Bengali food, though the two share a language and many dishes. `indian.md` covers West Bengal. This file covers Bangladesh and its diaspora, including the curry-house tradition that diaspora built.

**Native defaults:** Bangladeshi home curries are generally hotter, oilier and less sweet than Kolkata versions of the same names, with more onion, garlic and green chili. Heat comes from the dish itself and also from raw green chili eaten on the side.

## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these from the cuisine or the eater's name. Ask in Bangla (Bengali script) and get a clear answer.
- **Halal:** Bangladesh is Muslim-majority and home meat there is halal by custom, but a diaspora kitchen is not halal-certified just because it is Bangladeshi-run, and pork-free is not halal. Verify per kitchen. Ask: "Mangsho ki halal?" (মাংস কি হালাল?)
- **Beef is the everyday red meat**, used in tehari, bhuna, kala bhuna, mezban, haleem, nehari. "Mangsho" alone often means beef. Hindu Bangladeshi eaters, and many Hindu or Jain eaters generally, avoid beef. Ask: "Eta ki gorur mangsho, na khashir mangsho?" (এটা কি গরুর মাংস, না খাসির মাংস?). Khashi = castrated goat, the meat in proper kacchi.
- **Fish and dried fish hide in "vegetable" dishes:** lau chingri (gourd with shrimp), shak with dried fish, bhorta made with shutki, dal with fish head (muri ghonto). Ask: "Ete ki mach, chingri ba shutki ache?" (এতে কি মাছ, চিংড়ি বা শুঁটকি আছে?)
- **Vegetarian = niramish**, but confirm explicitly. In Hindu households niramish often also means no onion or garlic; ask if that matters: "Doya kore niramish korben: mach, mangsho, dim ba shutki diben na." (দয়া করে নিরামিষ করবেন: মাছ, মাংস, ডিম বা শুঁটকি দিবেন না।) Ghee is common in polao, khichuri and festive dishes.
- **Mustard** (oil, paste, kasundi) is in a large share of dishes. It is a regulated allergen in some places. If it matters, ask: "Ete ki shorisha ba shorishar tel ache?" (এতে কি সরিষা বা সরিষার তেল আছে?)
- **Meats many eaters don't expect (gloss them):** bhuri (tripe), kolija (liver), mogoj (brain), nehari (in Dhaka, a long-simmered stew of beef or goat paya (trotters) and shank, sold mostly as a breakfast or late-night dish; close to, but not always the same as, Pakistani nihari), nalli (marrow bone), chhagol vs khashi (goat vs castrated goat). Loitta = Bombay duck, a fish. Shutki = dried fish, strongly aromatic.
- **Ramadan:** many kitchens switch to iftar items (piyaju, beguni, chola, haleem, jilapi) and change hours. Check; don't assume.

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Kacchi biryani**: home (Dhaka) = raw marinated khashi (goat) layered with half-cooked rice and potato, sealed and cooked dum, with ghee, kewra, saffron and sometimes prunes; mild (heat 2–3), served with borhani and salad. Export = chicken or pre-cooked meat ("pakki"), no potato, food coloring, or a curry-house "biryani" that is fried rice with a separate curry sauce. Must: "kacchi, khashi, with aloo". Kolkata biryani also has potato but is a different, sweeter, lighter style; name which.
- **Tehari**: beef and rice, Dhaka-style in mustard oil with green chili; not biryani. Ghee and kewra vary by recipe, so don't treat them as a wrong substitution, but don't accept a biryani in its place.
- **Bhuna**: home = meat cooked down until the oil separates and the masala clings, little gravy. Curry-house "bhuna" is a medium sauce. Ask for it well bhuna.
- **Jhol vs bhuna vs dopiaza**: jhol is a thin light curry (fish, potato); bhuna is dry; dopiaza is onion-heavy. Name which.
- **Ilish**: home = bony whole-fish steaks, fried in mustard oil (the oil goes on rice) or in shorshe (mustard paste) with green chili. Export kitchens may swap in another fish or serve it boneless; check the menu description. Ilish is seasonal and often frozen abroad.
- **Bhorta**: home = mashed by hand with raw mustard oil, onion, green or burnt dried chili, salt. Without the raw mustard oil it is just mash. Ask for it.
- **Shatkora curries**: the rind goes into the curry; it is bitter-sour and can't be replaced by lime. If the kitchen has no shatkora, the dish isn't the dish.
- **Dal**: home = thin (patla) musur dal with a fried-garlic or panch phoron tempering. Don't accept a thick dal makhani-style substitute.
- **Mezbani gosht**: a feast dish ("mezban" = host's feast for guests), beef in mustard oil and a mezbani spice blend, oily and deep red; mezbani dal is its partner. It is known as a spicy dish, but recipes vary in how much dried chili they use, so the table's 5–6 is an estimate; take the kitchen's normal level and don't push it.
- **Curry-house dishes**: phall (the hottest tier, reportedly created in Bangladeshi-run Birmingham curry houses, often dated to 1971), British vindaloo (generic hot curry, often with potato; unrelated to Goan vinha d'alhos), chicken tikka masala (origin disputed: Glasgow vs a Punjab claim), korma and pasanda (sweet, creamy, coconut), Madras, balti, dhansak in its curry-house form (lentil-based, sweet-sour, often with pineapple standing in for the Parsi original's tamarind). This is a real export tradition with its own fans and fidelity markers, but it is not Bangladeshi home food. A Homecoming Bangladeshi eater usually wants the "deshi" dishes instead. An eater who asks for "curry-house vindaloo" wants that, not a Goan or Bangladeshi dish.
- **Breads**: home bread is rice-first; paratha (often Old Dhaka layered) for breakfast or with kala bhuna, bakarkhani with tea, chal-er ruti (rice-flour roti) with beef in Sylhet and Chittagong. Naan is a curry-house carb.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Kitchens cooking for outsiders run a shared base gravy across a fixed curry-house menu, add cream, sugar and coconut powder, swap goat for chicken, make biryani as fried rice plus sauce, drop the raw mustard oil and dried fish, and express "spicy" as a menu tier of chili powder (Madras → vindaloo → phall) rather than the dish's own spice. Correct back to the home levels in the table. Don't push past them; a homecoming kacchi is mild.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** write in standard Bangla, in Bengali script. Sylheti and Chittagonian (Chatgaiya) are distinct spoken varieties, often not mutually intelligible with standard Bangla in speech, but their speakers read standard written Bangla; don't attempt to spell Sylheti or Chittagonian. Never write to a Bangladeshi kitchen in Urdu (the 1952 Language Movement and 1971 make this a real offense) or in Hindi/Devanagari. Prefer Bangladeshi usage over Kolkata usage where they differ: pani (water), kacha morich (green chili; Kolkata says kancha lonka), shorishar tel (mustard oil; Kolkata shorsher tel), diben (Kolkata and standard written Bangla: deben). Both are understood; the Bangladeshi forms read as home. Fallback: English plus dish names.

**Sensitivities:** don't call the food or the language "Indian"; don't tell a Bangladeshi kitchen to cook "Kolkata style" unless the eater asked for exactly that. Bangal vs Ghoti (East vs West Bengal) is a friendly rivalry with real flavor differences: East is hotter and less sweet.

**Address default:** no address term. Greeting by provenance: "Assalamu alaikum" for Muslim-run kitchens (most), "Nomoshkar" for Hindu-run; English "Hello" if unknown. Use "bhai" (ভাই) or "apa" (আপা) only when the cook's gender is known. Never use junior-marking terms ("chhoto bhai", "beta", "tui").

## Meal composition norms
Rice is the base: about 1 rice per person, plus several small dishes rather than one big main. A home spread for two is dal + one bhorta or bhaji + one fish + one meat, with green chili, lemon and sliced onion-cucumber. Kacchi or tehari is a meal on its own; add borhani and salad, and a firni or zorda if wanted. Kala bhuna and Old Dhaka dishes pair with paratha. Curry-house ordering (one curry each + pilau + naan + poppadoms) is its own norm; use it only when the brief is curry-house.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- A "Bangladeshi specials" or "deshi" section separate from the curry-house list: shatkora, bhorta, shutki, ilish, kala bhuna.
- Kacchi offered as khashi with aloo; borhani on the menu.
- Bangla-script signage, menu or reviews; Bangladeshi festival specials (Eid, Pohela Boishakh panta-ilish, iftar items in Ramadan).
- Halal claims are common but not proof; if the brief needs halal, verify it per kitchen.
