## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these; ask. Peruvian kitchens are rarely halal or kosher.
- **Pork-free is not halal.** Pork dishes: carapulcra, adobo arequipeño, chicharrón, tamales (often pork, usually made with manteca de cerdo/lard), chancho con tamarindo. Ask: "¿Lleva cerdo o manteca de cerdo?" For halal: "¿La carne es halal?" (expect no).
- **Alcohol in cooking:** seco is often braised with chicha de jora or beer; lomo saltado is sometimes flambéed with pisco. Ask: "¿Se cocina con cerveza, chicha o pisco?"
- **Unexpected meats (gloss for the eater):** anticuchos = beef heart; cau cau / mondonguito = tripe; chanfainita = beef lung; cuy = guinea pig.
- **"Vegetarian" is not vegan:** papa a la huancaína and ocopa contain queso fresco and evaporated milk; causa often has mayonnaise (egg). Vegetable dishes may be cooked in chicken stock, and Chifa veg dishes often use oyster sauce. Ask: "¿Lleva caldo de pollo, salsa de ostión, queso o huevo?"
- **Seafood/shellfish:** leche de tigre is fish-based; ceviche mixto, chupe de camarones, choros a la chalaca are shellfish. Ask: "¿Lleva mariscos?"
- **Gluten:** soy sauce in saltados and Chifa; bread or crackers thicken ají de gallina. Ask: "¿Lleva sillao o pan?"

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Ceviche**: home = made to order, marinated only minutes in fresh lime with ají limo, red onion and leche de tigre; clearly sour, moderately spiced; served with camote and choclo/cancha. Export = marinated for hours or made ahead, and often with the ají left out. Both are fidelity failures.
- **Pollo a la brasa**: home = the cremas are the soul; ají de pollería (huacatay-based) and crema de rocoto come on the side, with papas fritas and ensalada. Export often swaps in a jalapeño-cilantro "ají verde". Ask for the real cremas on the side; more ají only on the eater's word.
- **Lomo saltado**: home = wok-seared (flambéed) beef with soy, tomato wedges and red onion; fries tossed in or on top, AND white rice on the side.
- **Ají de gallina**: home = shredded chicken in ají amarillo sauce thickened with bread/galleta, evaporated milk and pecans; served with rice, boiled potato, egg and olive. Export often turns it into a cream sauce.
- **Causa**: home = cold yellow-potato mash seasoned with ají amarillo and lime, layered with a filling (chicken, tuna).
- **Chifa agridulce / tamarindo**: home = sweet-sour by design. If the eater finds it too sweet, ask for "menos dulce", never "sin azúcar".

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Kitchens cooking for outsiders tend to drop the ají limo from ceviche, marinate it ahead, substitute jalapeño for Peruvian ajíes, thicken ají de gallina with cream, and serve pollo a la brasa without the real cremas.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** write notes in Peruvian Spanish. That covers Criollo, Andean, Chifa and Nikkei kitchens, whose cooks work in Spanish. Quechua and Aymara are home languages in the highlands but are not used for kitchen orders. Fallback: English plus dish names.

**Address default:** "¡Hola, buenas!" or "chef" / "para la cocina". "Maestro/maestra" only when the cook's gender is known.

"Harto" (a lot) and "al toque" (right away) are natural in Peruvian Spanish (both shared with neighbors); e.g. "ceviche recién hecho, al toque". "Al momento" is pan-Spanish, not a marker.

## Meal composition norms
Ceviche is a lunch dish. For two people: one ceviche, one hot dish (lomo saltado or ají de gallina), and rice. Pollo a la brasa: a quarter or half chicken per person, with fries and salad.
