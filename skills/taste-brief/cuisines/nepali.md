# Nepali: profile

## Traditions (never treat the country as one cuisine)
| Tradition | Signature dishes | Flavor signature | Native defaults | Staple carb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Everyday / hill (Khas) meals | dal bhat tarkari, masu (goat/chicken curry), saag, aloo tama | timur (Sichuan pepper relative), jimbu, mustard oil, achar on the side | heat 3–5 (side: achar 5–7) · sweet none · sour low (tama, tomato achar) · rich low–med | rice |

## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these. Ask, and record the answer.
- **"Buff" = water buffalo**, the default meat in valley momo, choila, sukuti and most Newari dishes. It is not cow beef, but some Hindu eaters avoid it too. Ask which meat: "Yo kun masu ho: khasi, kukhura ki rango?" (यो कुन मासु हो: खसी, कुखुरा कि राँगो?) — goat, chicken or buffalo.
- **Pork (bangur/sungur)** is common in Newari, Tamang, Gurung, Rai and Limbu cooking. Pork-free is not halal. Ask: "Yasma bangur ko masu chha?" (यसमा बंगुरको मासु छ?) and, separately, "Yo masu halal ho?" (यो मासु हलाल हो?). Most Nepali kitchens are not halal.
- **Ghee (ghiu) or butter (makkhan) in veg dishes**: Thakali sets and many dals and tarkaris use ghee. For dairy-free: "Ghiu ra makkhan nahalnuhos." (घिउ र मक्खन नहाल्नुहोस्।)
- **Unexpected meats, gloss them**: kachila is raw spiced minced buffalo; sapu mhicha is buffalo leg-marrow wrapped in tripe; bhutan is fried offal; sukuti is dried meat (buffalo or goat); samay baji includes buffalo, egg and often fried fish (machha). Thukpa or sha phaley may use yak or buffalo.
- **Fasting / vrat**: some Hindu eaters avoid onion and garlic, or all meat and eggs, on fast days. Ask: "Pyaj ra lasun nahalnuhos." (प्याज र लसुन नहाल्नुहोस्।)
- **"Veg" momo**: confirm no meat broth in jhol momo or thukpa: "Jhol ma masu ko jhol chha?" (झोलमा मासुको झोल छ?)

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Momo**: home = mild filling, served with a tomato-timur-sesame achar at a usual heat of 5–7 with a timur tingle. Variants are **jhol momo** (in a soup-achar) and **C-momo** (chili-tossed); name which. Export = a ketchup-like or sweet-chili dip instead of achar. Restore the achar at its usual level.
- **Dal bhat**: home = a set (dal, rice, tarkari, saag, achar, sometimes meat), with refills normal. Export = rice and one curry, or an Indian-style plate.
- **Achar carries the heat.** The plate itself is moderate. Ask for achar at its usual level, on the side; more heat only on the eater's word.
- **Gundruk** (fermented greens) and **timur** are identity markers. A kitchen that has them is in-culture.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Kitchens cooking for outsiders swap achar for sweet dips, drop timur and jimbu, use neutral oil instead of mustard oil, sweeten tomato achar, and pad the menu with creamy North Indian curries. Correct these back to the home baseline; do not push past it.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** Nepali works for most Nepali-run kitchens. Newar kitchens may speak Nepal Bhasa (Newari) at home but take orders in Nepali; a Newari greeting (Jwajalapa, ज्वजलपा) is a courtesy, not required. Tibetan-run Himalayan kitchens may prefer Tibetan or English over Nepali; don't assume. Fallback: English plus dish names.

**Address default:** open with Namaste and use the polite -nuhos verb forms. Use dai/didi only when the cook's gender is known. Never use bhai/bahini (younger sibling) for a cook.

## Meal composition norms
Dal bhat set: one per person. Momo: about 10 per person as a meal, or 5 as a starter. Choila, sekuwa and sukuti sadeko are drinking snacks; pair them with beaten rice.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- A dal bhat set and momo served with proper achar.
- Sekuwa, choila, gundruk or timur anywhere on the menu.
- Nepali-language reviews.
- Many "Indian" kitchens abroad are Nepali-run and will cook Nepali food on request. Check the market file.
