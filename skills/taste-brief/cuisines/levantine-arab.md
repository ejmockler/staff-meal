Scope: Mashriq, Egypt, Gulf, Yemen. North African (Moroccan, Algerian, Tunisian, Libyan) → maghrebi.md; Sudanese has no profile yet.

Desserts across the region (knafeh, luqaimat, bint al-sahn, basbousa) are sweet: high at home. Reduce only on the eater's word, and ask for "less", not "none".

## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these from the cuisine. An Arab kitchen is not automatically halal (some Christian-run kitchens, bars and mixed menus serve pork or cook with wine), and pork-free ≠ halal (halal also concerns how the meat was slaughtered).
- **Halal**: ask directly. Levantine/Egyptian: "law samaḥtu, el-laḥme ḥalāl?" (لو سمحتوا، اللحمة حلال؟).
- **Ghee/butter/lamb fat in "vegetarian" dishes**: samneh (ghee) goes into rice, fatteh, ful and pastries; liyye (lamb-tail fat) into kebab and kibbeh. Ask: "law samaḥtu, bidūn samneh w bidūn zibdeh" (لو سمحتوا، بدون سمنة وبدون زبدة).
- **Meat stock in "vegetarian" dishes**: molokhia, rice and freekeh are often cooked in chicken or meat broth. Ask: "law samaḥtu, bidūn maraq laḥme aw djēj" (لو سمحتوا، بدون مرق لحمة أو جاج). Lebanese "bi-zeit" (بالزيت) dishes are olive-oil-based and usually meat-free.
- **Christian fasting (Coptic, Orthodox)**: Egyptian "ṣiyāmi" (صيامي) means no animal products; fish is allowed on some fasts. Ask: "law samaḥtu, ṣiyāmi?" (لو سمحتوا، صيامي؟).
- **Sesame**: tahini, tarator, za'atar and many breads. Ask: "'andi ḥasāsiyye, bidūn ṭḥīneh w bidūn simsim" (عندي حساسية، بدون طحينة وبدون سمسم).
- **Wheat/gluten**: bulgur in tabbouleh and kibbeh; freekeh; vermicelli in rice.
- **Seafood**: zibdiyit gambari (shrimp), sayadieh, masgouf (fish).
- **Meats many eaters don't expect (gloss them)**: kibbeh nayyeh (raw lamb), mumbar / fawaregh (stuffed intestines), kawari' (trotters), pacha (Iraqi sheep's head and trotters), nikhā'/mukh (brains), kalawi (kidneys), hamam mahshi (stuffed pigeon, Egyptian), molokhia with arnab (rabbit).

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Shawarma (Lebanese/Syrian)**: home baseline: chicken (djej) = thin bread, toum, pickles, often fries inside; beef/lamb (lahmeh) = tarator (tahini), tomato, parsley-onion with sumac, pickles. No lettuce or cheese in either. Export versions (gyro-style, salad-heavy, mixed sauces) differ.
- **Falafel**: Egyptian ta'ameya is 100% fava and green inside from dill/parsley/coriander/leek; Levantine falafel is chickpea (Lebanese often chickpea+fava). Name which; don't swap one for the other on an Egyptian brief.
- **Hummus**: home = smooth and generous with tahini, and warm topped versions are common. Export hummus is often grainy and cold.
- **Heat lives on the side**: plates are mostly mild to moderate at home; heat comes from shatta (Egypt, Levant), sahawiq / سحاوق (Yemen; Western menus write 'zhug'), dakkus (Gulf), or Aleppo pepper / فلفل حلبي. Default: native plate, with the usual condiment on the side ("shatta on the side", "sahawiq on the side"). Ask for more only on the eater's word.
- **Knafeh and syrup desserts**: home = soaked in syrup (qaṭr) and served warm. If the eater wants less sweet, ask for the syrup on the side, not no sugar.
- **Mandi**: rice should be smoky and the meat falling apart.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Kitchens cooking for outsiders tend to cut garlic, samneh and lamb fat, mute baharat and dried lime, serve hummus cold, build salad-heavy wraps with mixed sauces, and blend the menu into generic "Mediterranean" (Greek/Turkish crossover).

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** all rows are Arabic, but match the kitchen's dialect from its provenance: Levantine (Lebanese, Syrian, Palestinian, Jordanian), Egyptian, Iraqi, Gulf, Yemeni. Levantine and Egyptian are understood almost everywhere; written Modern Standard Arabic is a safe but stiff fallback. Iraqi or Syrian kitchens run by Kurds may prefer Kurdish (Kurdish/Arabic is a sensitive pair); if unsure, use English plus dish names. Assyrian/Chaldean Iraqi kitchens are fine in Arabic.

**Address default:** "law samaḥtu" (plural "please") and "ya'ṭīkon el-'āfye" address the whole kitchen with no gender. Avoid khayyo / ya rayyes in written notes (male-assumed, overfamiliar); m'allem only if the cook is known to be the male owner/chef.

## Meal composition norms
Mezze is shared, 4–6 small plates for two, with plenty of bread. Grills come with rice or bread, pickles and toum. Mandi, mansaf and maqluba are centerpiece dishes sized for 2–4 people.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- An Arabic-script menu.
- Regional specificity (Aleppo, Gaza, Iraqi).
- Halal is necessary-not-sufficient (shared with Turkish, South Asian, Afghan kitchens); weight it below dialect-specific dishes such as ful/ta'ameya, mansaf, or a mandi pit.
- Toum and pickles served as standard.
