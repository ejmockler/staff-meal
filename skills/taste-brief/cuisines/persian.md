# Persian (Iranian): profile

Heat is low in most traditions; the South runs hotter. Fidelity is about **rice technique, saffron, sourness and herbs**, not chili.

## Traditions (never treat the country as one cuisine)
| Tradition | Signature dishes | Flavor signature | Native defaults | Staple carb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tehran / central | chelo kabab (koobideh, barg, joojeh, soltani), ghormeh sabzi, gheymeh, fesenjan, dizi/abgoosht, ash reshteh, kashk-e bademjan, zereshk polo ba morgh, baghali polo ba mahicheh, tahdig | saffron, butter, dried lime (limoo amani), fenugreek-herb, sumac, kashk | heat 0 (side: raw onion, torshi) · sweet low (fesenjan, zereshk polo: med) · sour med · rich med | chelo/polo, sangak, barbari, lavash |
| Gilan / Caspian | mirza ghasemi, baghala ghatogh, sour fesenjan, kateh | garlic, sour pomegranate, herbs | heat 1 (side: none) · sweet none · sour high · rich med | kateh rice |
| Azeri / Tabriz | kufteh Tabrizi, dolma | herbs, rice, split pea | heat 0 (side: none) · sweet low · sour low · rich med | rice |
| Southern / Persian Gulf (Bushehr, Hormozgan, Khuzestan) | ghalieh mahi, ghalieh meygoo (shrimp), meygoo polo, mahi Bandari, dopiazeh, Ahvazi falafel and sambuseh | tamarind, fenugreek, cilantro, chili, Gulf spice mix | heat 4 (side: chili sauce with falafel/sambuseh) · sweet none · sour high · rich med | rice |

## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
- **Halal is not guaranteed.** Iranian cooking is pork-free by default, but pork-free is not halal slaughter. Ask: "ببخشید، گوشتش حلاله؟" (bebakhshid, gooshtesh halāle?).
- **"Vegetarian" dishes may carry animal fat or stock.** Ash reshteh and some polos can use meat or chicken stock; rice and kashk-e bademjan are often finished with butter or roghan-e heyvāni (animal ghee). Ask: "لطفاً بدون گوشت و بدون آب گوشت یا آب مرغ باشه" (lotfan bedoon-e goosht va bedoon-e āb-e goosht yā āb-e morgh bāshe) and "ببخشید، کره یا روغن حیوانی داره؟" (bebakhshid, kare yā roghan-e heyvāni dāre?).
- **Not vegan.** Kashk (fermented whey) tops ash reshteh and kashk-e bademjan; mirza ghasemi has egg; koobideh rice often gets butter and sometimes raw egg yolk. Ask: "کشک یا تخم‌مرغ داره؟" (kashk yā tokhm-e morgh dāre?).
- **Meat in herb stews.** Ghormeh sabzi and gheymeh always contain lamb or beef; they are not vegetarian.
- **Tree nuts.** Fesenjan is a ground-walnut stew; many polos are garnished with pistachio or almond. State an allergy: "من به گردو حساسیت دارم" (man be gerdoo hassāsiyat dāram).
- **Shellfish.** Southern meygoo dishes are shrimp.
- **Unexpected meats (gloss them):** kaleh pacheh (sheep head and trotters), jigar (liver), del-o-jigar (heart and liver), sirābi (tripe), zabān (tongue), maghz (brain). Dizi and koobideh may include dombeh (lamb tail fat).

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Koobideh**: home = grilled tomato, raw onion and sumac on the side, butter (sometimes a raw egg yolk) on long-grain, saffron-streaked, fluffy rice; export = plain rice, no butter or sumac.
- **Tahdig**: home = the crisp bottom-pot rice is the prized part; export = often limited or a separate paid item. Ask for it explicitly.
- **Fesenjan**: home ranges from shirin (شیرین, sweet) through malas (ملس, sweet-sour, common in Tehran) to torsh (ترش, sour, Gilan); export = usually the sweetest end. Name which: "fesenjoon-e malas/torsh lotfan" (فسنجون ملس/ترش لطفاً). If the kitchen's version is sweet, ask for less sweet, not no sugar.
- **Khoresh**: home = thick and well-settled (jā-oftāde), sour from dried lime or herbs; export = thinner and less sour.
- **Herbs**: home = sabzi khordan (fresh herbs, radish, feta) comes with the meal; export = dropped.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Sweeter fesenjan and zereshk, less sourness overall, less saffron, short-grain or steamed rice with no tahdig, butter left off the rice, no sabzi khordan.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** Persian is the working language of nearly all Iranian kitchens, including Azeri, Gilaki, Kurdish and Khuzestani Arab cooks; a greeting in their own language is warm, but the request can stay in Persian. Afghan kitchens speak Dari: the script is the same, but don't use the Tehrani colloquial rows below; use standard written Persian or English plus dish names.

**Address default:** no address term needed. Open with "سلام، خسته نباشید" and use plural-polite verbs. Use آقا / خانم only when gender is known.

## Meal composition norms
Per person: one kabab or khoresh with chelo. Share a tahdig, a mast-o-khiar (yogurt-cucumber) or shirazi salad, and sabzi khordan. Doogh (a yogurt drink) is the pairing.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- Persian-script menu plus Iranian identity dishes: ghormeh sabzi, gheymeh, fesenjan, ash reshteh, dizi/abgoosht, kashk-e bademjan, zereshk polo. A Persian-script menu led by qabili palaw, mantu or bolani is Afghan, not Iranian.
- Tahdig and sabzi khordan as menu items; doogh and Iranian rice brands named.
