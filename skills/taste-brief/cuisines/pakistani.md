## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these from the cuisine. Ask in Urdu (Nastaliq) and get a clear answer.
- **Halal / zabiha:** common but not universal, and the absence of pork is not proof of halal. Verify per kitchen. Ask: "Kya gosht halal (zabiha) hai?" (کیا گوشت حلال / ذبیحہ ہے؟)
- **Beef is the default red meat** in nihari, haleem, bun kebab and shami kebab. "Bara gosht" (بڑا گوشت) = beef, "chhota gosht" (چھوٹا گوشت) = mutton/goat. This matters for eaters who avoid beef. Ask: "Yeh bara gosht hai ya chhota gosht?" (یہ بڑا گوشت ہے یا چھوٹا گوشت؟)
- **"Veg" is not vegetarian by default:** daal and sabzi are often cooked in desi ghee, sometimes with meat stock (yakhni); daal gosht, haleem and khichra contain meat; shami kebab is chana dal plus meat. Ask: "Kya is mein gosht ya gosht ki yakhni hai?" (کیا اس میں گوشت یا گوشت کی یخنی ہے؟) For no ghee: "Desi ghee ki jagah tel istemal kijiye." (دیسی گھی کی جگہ تیل استعمال کیجیے۔)
- **Dishes with meats many eaters don't expect (gloss them):** paye (trotters), siri (head), maghaz (brain), nalli (bone marrow), kaleji (liver), gurda (kidney), kapoora (testicles), kata-kat (mixed offal on a griddle).
- **Ramadan:** many kitchens close or cut the menu by day and open for iftar and sehri. Check hours; don't assume.

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Karahi**: home = cooked fresh to order in the karahi wok with tomato, green chili, ginger and ghee; no sugar, no cream. Export = ladled from a shared base gravy (the restaurant-curry shortcut), often with cream or sugar added. Ask for it fresh to order, no cream, no sugar.
- **Shinwari karahi**: home = deliberately low on spice: salt, lamb fat, tomato. Don't ask for heat on it.
- **Nihari**: home = slow overnight beef-shank stew (beef is the default; lamb/chicken are substitutes), with a thick fat layer (tari) and julienned ginger, green chili, lemon and fried onion on the side; add-ons "nalli" (marrow, نلی) and "maghaz" (brain, مغز). Eat with khamiri roti or naan. Export = thinner, less tari, garnish missing.
- **Biryani**: Karachi and Sindhi biryani differ (Sindhi adds potato and aloo bukhara and is more sour). Name which.
- **Naan**: home = plain large tandoori naan. The sweet coconut-raisin "Peshawari naan" is a British curry-house item, not Peshawari.
- **"Balti"** is a British-Pakistani invention. That's fine, but it isn't Pakistani home food.
- **Desi ghee** is the fat signal.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Kitchens cooking for outsiders tend to run a shared base gravy, add cream and sugar, tone down green chili and ginger, and mix in Indian curry-house dishes (tikka masala, korma). Correct back to the home levels in the table; don't push past them.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** default to Urdu, written in Nastaliq (Perso-Arabic) script, never Devanagari. Spoken Urdu and Hindi overlap, but use these lines, not the Indian file: never write "dhanyavaad", "bhaiya" or Devanagari. For Pashtun/Afghan-run kitchens (Shinwari, chapli, kabuli pulao), Urdu is understood, and a Pashto thanks is a warm touch. Fallback: English plus dish names.

**Address default:** open with the salaam and "kitchen team", no address term. Use "bhai" (بھائی) or "ustad" (استاد) only when the cook is known to be male. Never use junior-marking terms ("chhotu", "beta").

## Meal composition norms
Karahi is ordered by weight (half or full kg). A half kg serves two with 4–5 naan. Nihari and paye are breakfast or brunch. Biryani is a meal on its own, with raita and salad.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- Karahi sold by weight and cooked to order.
- Nihari or paye on the menu, and Urdu-script signage or reviews.
- Halal certification is common but not universal. If the brief needs halal, verify it per kitchen; don't infer it from the cuisine.
