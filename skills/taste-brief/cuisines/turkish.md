## Hard-filter terms (mandatory)
Never infer these; ask, and record the answer in `dietary:` as confirmed.
- **Pork-free ≠ halal.** Pork is near-absent from Turkish menus, but that does not make the meat halal-certified. Ask about certification if the eater needs it. Rakı or wine may also be served.
- **Unexpected meats (gloss them):** kokoreç = lamb intestines; mumbar = stuffed intestine; işkembe çorbası = tripe soup; kelle paça = head and trotters soup; ciğer = liver (Arnavut ciğeri, ciğer şiş); böbrek = kidney; uykuluk = sweetbreads; beyran = lamb soup with rice; sucuk = cured beef sausage.
- **Hidden meat in "plain" dishes:** mercimek çorbası, bulgur pilavı and rice pilav are often made with meat or chicken stock (et suyu / tavuk suyu). Dolma comes as zeytinyağlı (rice, usually vegan) or etli (with meat); name which.
- **Çiğ köfte:** the traditional version is raw meat. The commercial shop version is usually etsiz (meatless, bulgur-based). Ask which.
- **Butter in vegetable dishes:** pilav, mantı and many stews use tereyağı. Zeytinyağlı dishes use olive oil.
- **Fasting:** no dish rule, but during Ramazan kitchens may change hours around iftar.

## Home vs restaurant fidelity markers
- **Adana vs Urfa**: home = the same hand-minced kebab, Adana with chili worked into the meat (moderately hot), Urfa with almost none. Name which; don't treat Adana as "Urfa made hotter on request".
- **Döner**: home = et (beef/lamb) or tavuk (chicken), served ekmek arası or as dürüm with tomato, onion-parsley (sumac optional) and pickles; yogurt plus tomato butter makes it İskender, a plated dish. The diaspora döner-kebab sandwich (cabbage, several sauces, a big bread) is its own street-food tradition. **Yaprak döner** (stacked meat slices, not ground meat) is the quality signal.
- **Lahmacun**: home = paper-thin and crisp at the edges, eaten rolled with parsley and lemon; export = thick, pizza-like.
- **Pul biber / isot**: home = on the table, and each eater adds a pinch to taste. The dish itself stays at its native level; ask the kitchen for more only on the eater's word.
- **Baklava / künefe**: home = soaked in syrup and sweet. If the eater wants it less sweet, ask for less syrup (şerbeti az), never none.
- **Turkish coffee**: sugar is chosen at order time (sade, az şekerli, orta, şekerli); all are native.

## Export drift (generic; no named markets)
Ground-meat döner cones instead of yaprak; garlic and chili sauces in place of pul biber and sumac onion; leaner kebab without tail fat; thick lahmacun; machine-minced instead of zırh.

## Insider vocabulary (for chef notes)
**Language routing:** Turkish is the default. Many southeastern kitchens are run by Kurdish cooks; use Kurmanji only when the kitchen's provenance shows it, and never assume a Kurdish cook wants Turkish-only or vice versa. Hatay kitchens may also speak Arabic. Fallback: English plus Turkish dish names.

**Address default:** "Merhaba, kolay gelsin" (hello, may it come easy) needs no address term. "Usta" (master) is a respectful honorific for cooks and grill men, never junior-marking.

## Meal composition norms
Per person: one kebab plate (comes with bulgur or rice pilav, grilled pepper and tomato, lavaş), plus ezme, sumac-onion salad and ayran. Optional: mercimek çorbası to start, künefe or baklava to share after. For two people, add a meze plate or lahmacun to share.

## In-culture tells (market-agnostic; market specifics live in `kitchen-scout/markets/`)
- Yaprak döner and a charcoal mangal.
- Hand-minced (zırh) kebab, and regional pide or lahmacun.
- Turkish-language reviews.
